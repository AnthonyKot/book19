# Ideation — certified object (seed D) — Fable 5.1, 2026-09-04

Seed: what a building, dwelling, vehicle, vessel, installation or piece of equipment in the Netherlands must
carry a certificate, label, inspection or registration for before it can be sold, let, insured, connected, put
on the road or water, or kept in use; the moment an owned thing becomes unsellable or uninsurable without a
piece of paper, and who is short of the people who issue it. Worked from knowledge first; verification budget
twenty-five fetches, spent only on precedents, kill facts and the one number each kept idea depends on.
Excerpts under `sources/`. Off the table: everything kept, held, watched or cut in the three triage notes and
the two earlier ideation folders (assembled-machines, crossing-flows). Nothing below renames those.

A note on the seed before the list. Most of what this seed names is gated by a Dutch-language personal
certificate (EP-adviser, SCIOS, asbestos DIA, APK, BRL 6010, CO-certification, TCVT, CCV) or by designated
bodies that no small firm can become (lifts, pressure equipment, playgrounds). The book has already handled the
buyable ones as the certified-inspection class (#9). What is left, and what this run kept, are the three
shortages that no exam guards: electricians, structural engineers and energy auditors, each attached to a
dated rule.

## 3. Kept ideas (three)

### A. The car-park and depot charging file

1. **Iron plus paper.** Chargers on an SME car park or van depot cannot be connected or used without a grid
   application (a non-firm contract in congested areas), a load-management design that fits the chargers
   behind the existing connection, a NEN 1010 commissioning report and the Bbl compliance record; DC units of
   50 kW and more add the AFIR card-payment retrofit.
2. **Dated rule.** EPBD recast 2024/1275 Art. 14: all non-residential buildings with more than 20 parking
   spaces by 1 January 2027 (verified); the one-per-ten ratio and the transposition text are unverified;
   Dutch Bbl today: one point since 1 January 2025 (verified, art. 3.87b–c); vans out of the zero-emission
   zones from 1 January 2028 (from memory).
3. **Buyers.** Owner-occupied halls, workshops and depots of 10–100 staff, CEE-owned transport and
   construction firms first; incumbents are Dutch installers and charge-point operators who sell the
   operator contract, not the file.
4. **Edge.** Reads the Bbl transposition and the grid code; measures the site's spare capacity and designs
   load management from it; Polish electricians (NEN 3140 designation courses exist in Polish). No exam.
5. **Per unit.** €1,500–3,000 per site file (estimate); first test three sites through one Polish agency's
   clients, about €4,500; capital under €10k. Step two: a Dutch installer with grid-operator standing. Step
   three: own and operate the chargers, back-end outsourced.
6. **AI.** AI loads deepen grid congestion, so every car park needs a measured, load-managed design instead
   of a catalogue order, and the measurement is a visit.
7. **Kill fact.** The Dutch transposition exempts buildings owned and occupied by SMEs, as Germany's GEIG
   does (verified for Germany, unverified for the EU text and for the Bbl): then the owner-occupier owes
   nothing and the landlord market is Dutch and large.
8. **Precedent.** AMP Renewables Ltd, Washington (UK), Ltd since 2015, trading since 1985, NICEIC contractor:
   workplace and fleet charging with the DNO/G99 application, load balancing and the OZEV grant file, for
   "small offices (2–5 chargers)" up to depots, at £1,000–2,500 per charger installed (verified, own site plus
   Companies House 09420963). Caveat: a general contractor, and UK demand is grant-assisted.

**Facts a research run must settle.**
1. Exact Article 14(3) wording: the ratio per ten spaces and whether Member States may exempt buildings owned and occupied by SMEs. (EUR-Lex PDF via curl; Commission annex-9 guidance.)
2. Has the Netherlands transposed Article 14 (deadline 29 May 2026), and does the Bbl amendment keep or add an SME exemption? (Staatsblad; internetconsultatie.nl; iplo.nl.)
3. How many Dutch non-residential buildings have more than 20 parking spaces, by owner type? (RVO impact assessment of the 2020 Bouwbesluit amendment; CBS.)
4. Share of those owned and occupied by firms of 10–100 staff versus rented from funds. (Same sources; Kadaster.)
5. Grid operators' terms for chargers behind an existing connection in congested areas (non-firm contracts, capacity limits). (Netbeheer Nederland; Liander and Stedin pages.)
6. Do charger makers or CPOs bundle grid application and load-management design at no separate price? (Three quotes.)
7. AFIR Art. 5 retrofit population owned by SMEs, and the OEM retrofit-kit price. (Nationale Agenda Laadinfrastructuur; Alpitronic, ABB.)
8. Enforcement: which municipalities have inspected the 2025 one-point rule and with what result? (Municipal enforcement reports; WOO request.)
9. Does the 2028 van ban in the zero-emission zones still stand after the 2025–26 political revisions? (Rijksoverheid ZE-zones page.)
10. Professional liability cover for a one-person firm signing commissioning files. (Two brokers.)

### B. The plant energy file

1. **Iron plus paper.** A plant, depot or cold store kept in use owes an energy audit above 10 TJ a year, all
   carriers counted (a haulier with roughly ten trucks is in, on diesel alone: estimate), the four-yearly
   energy-saving report to the omgevingsdienst, and building automation above 290 kW. The audit is a walk
   through the plant with a clamp meter and a sub-metering plan.
2. **Dated rule.** EED 2023/1791 Art. 11: first audit by 11 October 2026, certified management system above
   85 TJ by 11 October 2027 (both verified). The Netherlands had not implemented the 10 TJ test at fetch date
   (verified, RVO), so the Dutch deadline is late and its form open. Informatieplicht round 1 December 2027
   and BACS above 290 kW since 1 January 2026: from memory.
3. **Buyers.** CEE-owned food plants, metal shops, hauliers and cold stores of 10–100 staff; incumbents are
   Dutch energy consultancies priced for the large-enterprise audit of the old rule.
4. **Edge.** No personal certificate is stated for EED auditors in NL (RVO page, verified by absence);
   reads the directive and the Erkende Maatregelenlijst; builds the evidence trail; the buyers' language.
5. **Per unit.** €3,000–8,000 per site audit, €500–1,500 per energy-saving report (estimates); first test one
   Polish-owned plant, about €4,000; capital under €5k. Step two: a Dutch partner for the omgevingsdienst.
   Step three: install the measures (metering, drives, heat recovery) or buy a small installer.
6. **AI.** Automation adds kilowatts to the same plant and electrification pushes more sites over 10 TJ,
   and each new site is a visit, not a dataset.
7. **Kill fact.** The Dutch implementation lets the existing onderzoeksplicht or informatieplicht report
   satisfy Article 11, so no firm between 10 TJ and the old threshold buys anything new.
8. **Precedent.** DeltaUWB GmbH (Ingenieurbüro Schwark), Blankenhain, Thuringia: founded 2016, GmbH since
   2022, second office in Gotha, a family team of engineers and master craftsmen selling DIN EN 16247 audits
   to "producing Mittelstand" (metal, textile, bakeries) and municipalities (verified, own site plus two
   directory listings; BAFA list not fetched). Caveat: German SME audits are BAFA-subsidised; the Dutch
   buyer pays in full.

| unit price | €3,000–8,000 per audit; €500–1,500 per report | estimate, unverified |
| incumbent price | not found; German BAFA subsidy cap €3,000 per audit (precedent) | verified for the subsidy only |
| entry cost | under €5k (clamp meters, loggers, templates) | estimate |
| capital tied up | under €5k | estimate |

**Facts a research run must settle.**
1. Where is the Dutch implementation of Article 11 (bill, consultation, in-force date), and does it use 10 TJ or another test? (Rijksoverheid; internetconsultatie.nl; RVO.)
2. Does the Dutch rule count transport fuel, so hauliers fall in? (Implementing text; RVO Q&A.)
3. How many Dutch enterprises sit between 10 TJ and the old large-enterprise test, by sector? (CBS energy-use by enterprise size; RVO impact assessment.)
4. Is any auditor qualification required or planned in NL, and is it obtainable in English? (RVO; the implementing text.)
5. Can an existing onderzoeksplicht report be accepted as the EED audit? (RVO template "Onderzoeksplicht + EED-auditplicht".)
6. What do Dutch advisers charge a 40-staff plant for an audit today? (Three quotes.)
7. RVO's rejection rate of submitted audit reports and what fails. (RVO annual figures; WOO request.)
8. Enforcement: omgevingsdienst inspection counts on the energy-saving obligation 2024–26. (Omgevingsdienst NL reports.)
9. Overlap with the fleet-service desk (#12): are the same CEE-owned hauliers the buyers, and does that help or crowd? (Author's call.)
10. Does the BACS obligation above 290 kW bite in practice, and who checks it? (Bbl text; RVO.)

### C. The roof file

1. **Iron plus paper.** PV on an SME roof needs a structural roof-load calculation, the insurer's fire layout
   and Scope 12 pre-check, a grid application or zero-feed-in setting, and a self-consumption design once
   net metering ends. The Netherlands has no protected structural-engineer title, so a Polish engineer's
   calculation stands (from knowledge).
2. **Dated rule.** Net metering ends for households and small businesses on 1 January 2027 (verified, Eerste
   Kamer, 17 December 2024). EPBD Art. 10: solar on all new public and non-residential buildings above
   250 m² by 31 December 2026 (verified), further deadlines 2027 and 2029 (categories from memory).
3. **Buyers.** Owner-occupier SMEs putting PV on halls, and mid-size installers who subcontract the
   structural check; incumbents are Dutch engineering firms and the installers' bundled quotes.
4. **Edge.** Engineering habit; Polish structural engineers and PV crews as supply; reads the grid code and
   the insurers' conditions. No exam.
5. **Per unit.** €1,500–3,000 per roof file (estimate; the German precedent charges from €1,500); first test
   three roofs, about €4,500; capital under €10k. Step two: a Dutch installer partner. Step three: rooftop
   operator, owning the array and selling the power.
6. **AI.** Congestion from AI loads makes the self-consumption design the deciding document, and the roof
   survey is a person on a roof.
7. **Kill fact.** Dutch installers include the structural check and the Scope 12 pre-check in every B2B
   quote at no separate price. Unchecked; likely partly true.
8. **Precedent.** Gutachten Graf GmbH, Lohmar (NRW): a TÜV-certified PV expert selling acceptance
   inspections, structural and technical checks, yield analysis and construction supervision to commercial
   roof owners, "starting €1,500", industrial projects "€3,000–5,000+", supervision about 2% of the
   installation (verified, own site plus Northdata register extract; founding year not found). Caveat: the
   owner also runs an installer, GRAF.SOLAR GmbH.

**Facts a research run must settle.**
1. Do Dutch B2B PV quotes include the structural calculation and Scope 12 pre-check, and at what line price? (Five installer quotes.)
2. What do Dutch engineering firms charge for a PV roof-load check on a steel hall? (Three quotes.)
3. Is a calculation signed by a Polish engineer accepted by Dutch insurers and municipalities without a Dutch countersignature? (Verbond van Verzekeraars; two insurers; a Wkb quality assurer.)
4. Which categories carry the 31 December 2027 and 2029 solar deadlines, and how will the Bbl transpose them? (Directive text; Dutch consultation.)
5. What does the end of net metering do to the SME roof business case, by connection size? (PBL; Netbeheer Nederland.)
6. Grid operators' zero-feed-in and curtailment terms for new PV in congested areas. (Liander, Stedin, Enexis pages.)
7. Insurers' current PV conditions: Scope 12 timing, fire layout, module distance to roof edge. (Verbond checklist; NTA 8220.)
8. Number of Dutch non-residential roofs above 500 m² without PV, by owner type. (RVO "zon op dak" studies; CBS.)
9. Founding year and staff of Gutachten Graf GmbH, and two more German or UK independent PV experts, to test that the shape carries a firm. (Handelsregister; BDSF directory.)
10. Does the rooftop-operator step (own the array) fit the capital ceiling, or is it an acquisition? (Author's four constraints.)

## 5. Honest paragraph

This seed found nothing a Dutch founder with the same reading could not: every rule here is printed in
Dutch, and Dutch installers, engineers and advisers already sell each file. What it found is narrower: the
three shortages the seed points at that no Dutch exam guards (electricians, structural engineers, energy
auditors), each with a 2026–2027 date, and buyers the reader can phone in their own language. Vessels and
vehicles gave nothing in-seed. My money would go to B, the plant energy file: the EU date is five weeks away,
the Netherlands is late, no certificate stands in the way, and CEE-owned hauliers and plants are the reader's
own network.
