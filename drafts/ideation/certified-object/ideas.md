# Ideation — certified object (seed D) — Fable 5.1, 2026-09-04

Seed: what a building, dwelling, vehicle, vessel, installation or piece of equipment in the Netherlands must
carry a certificate, label, inspection or registration for before it can be sold, let, insured, connected, put
on the road or water, or kept in use; the moment an owned thing becomes unsellable or uninsurable without a
piece of paper, and who is short of the people who issue it. Worked from knowledge first; verification budget
twenty-five fetches, spent only on precedents, kill facts and the one number each kept idea depends on.
Excerpts under `sources/`. Off the table: everything kept, held, watched or cut in the three triage notes and
the two earlier ideation folders (assembled-machines, crossing-flows). Nothing below renames those.

A note on the seed before the list. Most of what this seed names is gated by a Dutch-language personal
certificate (EP-adviser, SCIOS, asbestos DIA, APK, BRL 6010, CO-certification, TCVT, CCV) or by designated
bodies that no small firm can become (lifts, pressure equipment, playgrounds). The book has already handled the
buyable ones as the certified-inspection class (#9). What is left, and what this run kept, are the three
shortages that no exam guards: electricians, structural engineers and energy auditors, each attached to a
dated rule.

## 2. Kept ideas (three, all unverified: no fetches were made before the stop)

### A. The car-park and depot charging file

1. **Iron plus paper.** Chargers on an SME car park or van depot: grid application (congested areas need a
   non-firm contract), load-management design so the chargers fit behind the existing connection, NEN 1010
   commissioning report, Bbl compliance record, and for DC units of 50 kW+ the AFIR card-payment retrofit.
2. **Dated rule.** EPBD recast 2024/1275 Art. 14(3): non-residential buildings with more than 20 parking spaces
   by 1 January 2027 (from memory, unverified: one point per ten spaces); AFIR Art. 5 retrofit by 1 January
   2027; Dutch zero-emission zones close to diesel vans 1 January 2028. Dutch transposition due 29 May 2026.
3. **Buyers.** Owner-occupied halls, workshops and depots of 10–100 staff, CEE-owned transport and
   construction firms included; incumbents are Dutch charging installers and CPOs who want the operator contract.
4. **Edge.** Reads the Bbl transposition; designs load management from a measured site; Polish electricians
   (NEN 3140 designation courses exist in Polish). No exam.
5. **Per unit.** €1,500–3,000 per car-park design-and-commissioning file (estimate); first test three sites
   for one Polish agency's clients, ~€4,500; capital <€10k (measuring, insurance). Step two: a Dutch
   grid-savvy installer partner. Step three: own and operate the chargers (hardware, outsourced back-end).
6. **AI.** AI loads deepen congestion, so every car park needs a measured load-managed design, not a catalogue.
7. **Kill fact.** The Dutch Bbl exempts buildings owned and occupied by SMEs (as under EPBD III), so the
   10–100-staff owner-occupier owes nothing and the landlord market is Dutch and large. UNCHECKED.
8. **Precedent to find.** German small firms selling GEIG (Gebäude-Elektromobilitätsinfrastruktur-Gesetz)
   compliance and workplace charging to the Mittelstand; UK OZEV-approved workplace installers. NOT FOUND YET.

### B. The plant energy file

1. **Iron plus paper.** A plant, depot or cold store kept in use owes: an EED energy audit (above 10 TJ a year,
   diesel counted, so a haulier with roughly ten trucks is in), the four-yearly energy-saving report to the
   omgevingsdienst, and a BACS above 290 kW. The audit is a walk with a clamp meter.
2. **Dated rule.** EED 2023/1791 Art. 11: first audit by 11 October 2026, EnMS above 85 TJ by 11 October 2027
   (from memory); Dutch informatieplicht round closes 1 December 2027; BACS since 1 January 2026.
3. **Buyers.** CEE-owned food plants, metal shops, hauliers, cold stores of 10–100 staff; incumbents are Dutch
   energy consultancies priced for large enterprises.
4. **Edge.** No personal certificate for EED auditors in NL (unverified); reads the directive and the EML;
   sub-metering and an evidence trail; the buyers' language.
5. **Per unit.** €3,000–8,000 per site audit, €500–1,500 per energy-saving report (estimates); first test one
   Polish-owned plant, ~€4,000; capital <€5k. Step two: Dutch partner for the omgevingsdienst. Step three:
   implement the measures (installed iron) or buy a small installer.
6. **AI.** Automation adds kW to the same plant and electrification pushes more sites over 10 TJ.
7. **Kill fact.** The Dutch implementation keeps the old large-enterprise test or lets the existing
   onderzoeksplicht satisfy Art. 11, so no new firms fall in. UNCHECKED.
8. **Precedent to find.** BAFA-listed German energy-audit microfirms (DIN EN 16247) selling to the Mittelstand;
   the BAFA register is the independent trace. NOT FOUND YET.

### C. The roof file

1. **Iron plus paper.** PV on an SME roof needs a structural roof-load calculation, an insurer's fire layout and
   Scope 12 pre-check, a grid application or zero-feed-in setting, and a self-consumption design once net
   metering ends. NL has no protected structural-engineer title, so a Polish engineer's calculation stands.
2. **Dated rule.** Net metering ends for all small connections 1 January 2027 (Eerste Kamer, Feb 2025, from
   memory); EPBD Art. 10 solar mandate: new non-residential >250 m² from 31 December 2026, major renovations
   from 31 December 2027.
3. **Buyers.** Owner-occupier SMEs and mid-size installers who subcontract the structural check.
4. **Edge.** Engineering habit; Polish structural engineers and PV crews as supply; reads the grid code. No exam.
5. **Per unit.** €1,500–3,000 per roof file (estimate); first test three roofs, ~€4,500; capital <€10k.
   Step two: Dutch installer partner. Step three: rooftop operator (own the array, sell the power).
6. **AI.** Congestion from AI loads makes self-consumption design the deciding file; the roof survey is a
   person on a roof.
7. **Kill fact.** Dutch PV installers include the structural check and Scope 12 pre-check in every B2B quote
   at no separate price. UNCHECKED; likely partly true.
8. **Precedent to find.** German independent PV-Sachverständiger microfirms (acceptance inspections, yield
   reports); UK PV acceptance testers. NOT FOUND YET.
