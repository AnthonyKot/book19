## 4. Additions

### A1. The extraction file — a thorough examination and test per extraction system — WATCH

1. **Iron plus paper.** The extraction hood, booth or bench that keeps a spray, welding, wood or
   composites shop under its limit, and the test report that says it does. In the UK this is a
   statutory record: "Must be carried out at least once every 14 months" under COSHH Regulation 9,
   guidance HSG258 (verified, Impact Technical Services). In the Netherlands the duty is the general one
   in Arbobesluit 4.5 (ventilation effective and checked), with no interval and no named tester
   qualification found (search, 2026-09-05; the AVAG page did not reproduce the article).
2. **Dated rule — indirect.** 9 April 2026 cuts the diisocyanate and lead limits so far that "the booth
   works" stops being a plausible claim in an RI&E and becomes a measured one; the Inspectorate expects
   the control to be shown effective. That is the same indirect link that put K3 on WATCH, and it
   is why A1 sits there too.
3. **Buyers.** Body shops, welding and metal shops, joinery and composites shops of 10–100 staff,
   including Polish- and Ukrainian-owned workshops (the assembled-machines run's modification-file
   buyers). Incumbents: extraction installers' service contracts (Lastraga-type), hygiene bureaus by
   the day.
4. **Edge.** Measurement habit (face velocity, static pressure, smoke, a report per hood); BOHS P601 is
   an English course (verified as the qualification cited; eligibility for a Dutch resident unverified);
   the reader explains the failed hood to the Polish welder.
5. **Per unit.** UK "from just £250" per single-point system (verified); Dutch estimate €300–450 per
   system. First test: twenty systems across four workshops, ~€3,000. Capital: anemometer, manometer,
   smoke generator, Pitot kit under €3,000 (estimate). Step two: a Dutch hygienist or installer partner.
   Step three: install and service extraction, the physical business the UK precedents also run.
6. **AI.** No model measures airflow at a hood; every new booth, welder and limit adds a test.
7. **Kill fact.** Dutch extraction installers already bundle the effectiveness check in maintenance
   contracts at no separate price, or the Arbobesluit's lack of an interval means nobody buys a test
   until the Inspectorate visits. Untested.
8. **Precedent.** Impact Technical Services Ltd, Leicester: LEV thorough examination and test, "from
   just £250", P601–P604, customers in welding, woodworking, composites, automotive; "30 years of
   nationwide coverage" (own site, 2026-09-05); Companies House 07532316, incorporated 16 February 2011,
   active, same address (2026-09-05). Nine UK firms in one search sell the same unit. Synergy (K1's
   precedent) sells it beside isocyanate monitoring, which is the shape proposed here: one desk, one
   crew visit, three files.

## 5. What the precedent test changed (≤120 words)

All three precedents held under a second trace, and that is the trap: each lives where the record is
a legal duty the Netherlands lacks. RPA tests faces under COSHH guidance; Impact tests hoods on a
14-month clock; EHS Analytical samples lead under OSHA. Only Synergy's unit, the isocyanate crew
survey, meets a Dutch rule of the same weight, from April 2026. So "a precedent exists" proved
necessary and not sufficient; the pass added a question to part 8: does the precedent's rule exist
here? The other change is part 4: the Inspectorate wants a hygienist-level assessor, which moves K1's
partner from step two to the first sale. Three ideas became one desk with three lines and a step-three
iron (extraction), and K1 is still the one to fund.
