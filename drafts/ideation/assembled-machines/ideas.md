# Ideation — assembled machines (seed A) — Fable 5.1, 2026-09-04

Seed: things Dutch small and mid-sized firms buy as equipment that become a regulated machine, installation or
product the moment they are assembled, installed, modified, connected or put on the EU market. Worked from
knowledge first; verification budget ten fetches, used only for kill facts and the one number each kept idea
depends on. Excerpts under `sources/`. Everything in the two triage notes is off the table, and anything whose
unit is a shipment or a person crossing a border belongs to the sibling seed (crossing-flows) and is not
proposed here.

## Kept ideas

### A. The cell file — CE for cells, lines and fleets assembled on the floor

1. **Iron plus paper.** A cobot cell, palletiser, AMR fleet, packing line or van tail lift assembled from CE-marked
   parts is a new machine; the assembler owes risk assessment, technical file and declaration before staff may use
   it.
2. **Dated rule.** Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027: assembler as manufacturer, a new
   anti-corruption requirement, and EN ISO 10218-2:2025 replacing the 2011 text, so old cell files need rewriting
   (all unverified).
3. **Buyers.** Dutch SME manufacturers of 10–100 staff buying first cobots, and small integrators, Polish ones
   included. Bureaus: €6,000 per machine, €30,000 per assembly (verified, IAB).
4. **Edge.** Reads the Regulation and standards; measures force and stopping distance into a templated evidence
   trail; Polish integrators as channel. No Dutch exam.
5. **Per unit.** €1,500–3,000 per cell, €4–8k per line (estimates). First test: three cells for one integrator,
   ~€4,500. Capital: measuring set €5–10k (estimate). Step two: Dutch safety engineer for instructions and RI&E.
   Step three: become the integrator who installs and signs.
6. **AI.** Every cheaper cobot is one more assembly; stopping distance is measured by a person holding the meter.
7. **Kill fact.** A cobot distributor bundles the cell CE free, or a bureau sells it at ≤€1,500.

**Research questions (likely source).**
1. Does Regulation 2023/1230 apply from 20 Jan 2027, and does Art. 3/10 make the assembler the manufacturer? (EUR-Lex PDF via curl.)
2. Is EN ISO 10218-2:2025 published and cited in the OJ under the Regulation, and by when? (ISO catalogue; OJ C series harmonised-standards list.)
3. How many industrial robots and cobots were installed in NL in 2024 and 2025? (IFR World Robotics 2025 country table; the search gave Europe 85,000, cobots ~12%, no NL figure.)
4. Do UR, Doosan or Fanuc Dutch distributors bundle the cell CE with installation, at what price? (Distributor sites; two phone calls.)
5. What do D-SC, Fusacon, Pilz NL, Perios and Olmia charge per cobot cell today? (Their sites; Olmia whitepaper in sources.)
6. Does the Dutch Labour Inspectorate treat an integrated cobot as a new machine under Arbobesluit art. 7.2–7.4? (Arboportaal; NLA inspection reports.)
7. What does an ISO/TS 15066 force-measurement set cost to buy or rent? (Pilz PRMS/GTE, Schmersal, rental firms.)
8. How many Polish integrators sell cells into NL, and do their Dutch customers ask for the file? (Polish robotics association member lists; UR partner map.)
9. Can a one-person Dutch firm get professional liability cover for signing CE files, and at what premium? (Two Dutch brokers.)
10. Under the Regulation, must the manufacturer sign the declaration, so the reader's file is always countersigned by the integrator? (Art. 10 and Annex V.)
11. How many Dutch pack-houses and food SMEs automated in 2024–25 (subsidy lists, VNO-NCW/FME surveys)?

### B. The old iron — the substantial-modification file for machines already on the floor

1. **Iron plus paper.** A machine in a Dutch workshop is retrofitted (new controls, robot loader, vision, higher speed)
   or recommissioned. If it adds a hazard, the modifier becomes manufacturer and owes new risk assessment, file and
   declaration; otherwise a signed memo.
2. **Dated rule.** The Machinery Regulation makes "substantial modification" binding law (Art. 3, 18) from
   20 January 2027; Product Liability Directive 2024/2853 (transposed 9 December 2026) makes the modifier liable as
   manufacturer (unverified).
3. **Buyers.** Polish- and Ukrainian-owned metal, wood and food workshops of 10–100 staff, Polish retrofit shops.
   Incumbents: €20,000 per large machine (verified, IAB).
4. **Edge.** Reads Art. 18 and the guide; a modification register answers "substantial or not" in an hour;
   Polish retrofit shops as channel. No Dutch exam.
5. **Per unit.** €900 memo, €2,500–5,000 re-CE (estimates). First test: five machines in one Polish-owned workshop,
   ~€4,500. Capital <€5k. Step two: Dutch partner for the Inspectorate. Step three: buy the retrofit shop and sign
   as modifier (fits triage #3).
6. **AI.** Retrofit is how AI enters old plants; each one is a question answered at the guard.
7. **Kill fact.** The definition is so narrow that like-for-like swaps never count; or inspectors never enforce on
   modified machines.

**Research questions.**
1. Exact wording of Art. 3 "substantial modification" and Art. 18 in 2023/1230. (EUR-Lex PDF.)
2. Does Directive 2024/2853 Art. 8 treat a substantial modifier as manufacturer, transposition 9 Dec 2026? (EUR-Lex.)
3. Has the Commission published Machinery Regulation guidance on which retrofits are substantial? (DG GROW machinery page; the 2006/42 Guide §72 as baseline.)
4. How many NLA inspection cases in 2023–25 concerned modified machines? (Nederlandse Arbeidsinspectie annual reports.)
5. What share of Dutch SME machine parks is pre-CE (before 1995) or pre-2010? (FME or Metaalunie surveys; CBS capital-stock age.)
6. How many Polish retrofit and controls shops serve Dutch customers? (Polish automation association; LinkedIn.)
7. What do Dutch bureaus charge for a modification assessment? (IAB, D-SC, Perios price pages.)
8. Is the used-machinery trade (a border question, sibling seed) already shipping "CE packages" with retrofits, undercutting the memo? (Polish dealer sites.)
9. Do Dutch insurers require a modification assessment after retrofit? (Insurer machine-breakdown conditions.)
10. Does the employer duty in Arbobesluit art. 7.4a require an inspection after modification, by whom? (Arboportaal.)

### C. The pack — CE and passport file for battery packs assembled in the Netherlands

1. **Iron plus paper.** A lithium pack assembled from cells for an electric boat, AGV, forklift retrofit or power unit.
   The assembler is the battery's manufacturer and owes CE, declaration, technical file, labelling, QR code and,
   above 2 kWh, a battery passport.
2. **Dated rule.** Regulation (EU) 2023/1542: labelling from 18 August 2026, QR code and passport from
   18 February 2027 (unverified); the product passport is in the reader's four books.
3. **Buyers.** Dutch electric-boat yards (dozens, 10–60 staff, English), AGV builders, forklift electrifiers, Polish
   pack assemblers. Labs sell testing-plus-CE at €10–30k per model (estimate), no passport.
4. **Edge.** Reads the Regulation and passport acts; templates cell-to-pack traceability; Polish assemblers as
   supply. No Dutch exam.
5. **Per unit.** €2,500–5,000 per pack model, €5–20 per pack for passport upkeep (estimates). First test: one
   yard's pack model, ~€4,000. Capital <€10k; testing is the client's. Step two: lab and passport provider as
   partners. Step three: buy a small pack assembler.
6. **AI.** Robots, AMRs and drones all carry packs; each model is a file with a physical test behind it.
7. **Kill fact.** The module supplier's passport is accepted as covering the pack; or the passport turns this into
   software within a year.

### D. The learning machine — notified-body file for AI safety functions (WATCH)

1. **Iron plus paper.** A safety function using machine learning (camera zone monitoring, self-tuning
   speed-and-separation) integrated into a cell. Under the Machinery Regulation such components and the machinery
   embedding them are Annex I Part A: no self-declaration, a notified body must examine the file.
2. **Dated rule.** 20 January 2027 (Machinery Regulation); AI Act product rules from 2 August 2027 (both
   unverified; the Digital Omnibus may move the second).
3. **Buyers.** Vision-safety startups and small integrators of 10–100 staff, in English, who must hand a notified
   body a complete file; notified bodies review, they do not prepare.
4. **Edge.** Reads both acts; builds the validation evidence for an installed cell. Warning: that evidence is data
   work, on the book's IT edge.
5. **Per unit.** €6–15k per application (estimate); first test a €4,000 gap analysis. Capital <€5k. Step two:
   preferred preparer for a Dutch notified body. Step three: unclear, hence WATCH.
6. **AI.** Literal: every learned safety function is a mandatory third-party file.
7. **Kill fact.** In 2027 no SME integrator uses machine learning in a safety function, because SICK, Pilz and
   peers certify their own components and integrators use those; demand is zero until about 2029.

## Honest paragraph

Nothing here is invisible to a Dutch founder with the same reading; Dutch CE bureaus already sell every one of
these files, and my only verified fact is what they charge. The reader's difference is narrow: a Polish and
Ukrainian integrator and workshop channel the bureaus do not call, a templated evidence trail that prices a cell
at a quarter of the incumbent, and reading the Regulation before the deadline. The seed's weakness is that it
starts as consulting; only step three (install and sign) makes it the book's business. My own money would go to
A, the cell file: real deadline, countable units, tools under €10,000, and a walk to becoming the integrator.
