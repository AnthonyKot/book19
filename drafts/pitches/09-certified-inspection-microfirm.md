# Pitch 9 — Buy the certified-inspection microfirm and keep the certificate-holder (Family B)

**The unit and the file.** One microbusiness whose product is a signed inspection that the law
requires and that only a certified person may sign: an asbestos survey firm (the DIA surveyor),
an emergency-generator inspection firm (SCIOS scope 4/7c), a pest-control firm (per-person
biocide competence). Sells inspections per site or per contract; the firm's value is the
certificate-holder's signature and the contract book.

**The rule and the date.** Asbestos surveys mandatory before renovation and demolition; SCIOS
generator inspection mandatory since 1 January 2016 on a two-year cycle above 100 kW (verified,
AI-footprint memo); biocide use requires per-person competence (verified). No new date; the
obligations are standing, which is the point: they do not expire.

**Who buys.** Building owners, contractors, data-centre and hospital facility managers, food and
housing operators. Demand is regulatory and steady; the incumbents are small and ageing; the pest
listing found shows about €240k revenue and €78k EBITDA on two to five staff, inside the
€100k-plus-Qredits envelope at the barometer's 3.6× (verified, boring-business memo).

**Why you.** Capital and the owner-independence rule from the succession memo; process discipline
for scheduling, evidence and reporting that these firms run on paper; CEE-speaking clients in
housing and food for pest control. The Dutch gate is the exam, which is Dutch-only in all three
cases and which the reader does not take: the certificate-holder stays, on paper, through the
change of control.

**The precedent.** The class is the precedent: three memos found the same deal shape in three
sectors. One listing (pest control, Brookz) is saved; the asbestos memo found a firm seeking
freelance surveyors in three cities (verified).

**The ninety-day test.** One overflow job brokered to a certified firm for a fee: proves the
demand and the relationship before any purchase. Under €200.

**Steps two and three.** Step two: buy one firm with the certificate-holder on a retention
agreement. Step three: a second certificate-holder hired, or a second firm in an adjacent
inspection class on the same back office; the AI build-out adds generator fleets as a customer.

**The AI line.** A survey is a person on a site with a sampling kit and a signature; AI writes
the report. Demand rises with regulation, and regulation is not falling.

**The kill fact.** No certificate-holder stays through a change of control at a price the
capital allows. It is answered in the first conversation with the seller.

**The number to settle first.** The retention cost of the certificate-holder against the firm's
EBITDA: if the signature costs half the profit, there is no deal.

**Verdict under the four assumptions.** Ranked ninth. Not beside a job after purchase; capital
is most of the ceiling; no retraining, because the reader never takes the exam. Rises if the
capital ceiling is higher; fails if the reader wants to be the one who signs.
