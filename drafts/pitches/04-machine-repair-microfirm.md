# Pitch 4 — Buy the machine-repair microfirm (Family B)

**The unit and the file.** A repair-and-installation firm for industrial machinery (SBI 33) with
two to ten staff, bought or built around one hired engineer; sells maintenance contracts, call-outs
and spares sourcing per plant. No broker category exists, so the file is the walk: industrial
estates, accountants, OEM service partners.

**The rule and the date.** No regulatory date. The data: 79.0% of 2018 starters alive in 2023
(80.6% with one to five staff), the lowest small-firm exit rate of any sector tested (1.9% in
2025), 17 bankruptcies on 14,175 firms, operating margin 8.6%, revenue per worker €256k, 2,080
firms in the two-to-ten class, 39% of industrial employers aged 55+ (all verified, CBS, with raw
rows saved).

**Who buys.** Plants and workshops in Dutch industry that cannot keep a maintenance engineer on
staff and whose OEM service is slow or priced for large customers. Plant managers work in
English; site safety briefings are in Dutch.

**Why you.** The one sector where the engineering habit is the edge without the business becoming
IT: fault-finding, documentation, predictive maintenance as field calls. Polish technicians and
parts supply on the CEE side. No Dutch exam for the owner; the technicians' certificates are
per person and stay with them.

**The precedent.** The sector itself is the precedent: the quietest survivor in the CBS series.
No single firm fetched; the boring-business memo's kill fact is that there may be no firm to
buy because the skill sits in the seller.

**The ninety-day test.** One paid maintenance-audit-plus-spares-sourcing job for an
English-speaking plant, sold through an existing repair firm as their subcontractor. Under €500.

**Steps two and three.** Step two: hire one Dutch-speaking engineer and take contracts in the
firm's name, or buy the firm of a retiring owner with the engineer retained. Step three: the
cell file (pitch 2) sold to the same plants, which turns a repair shop into the integrator that
installs and signs.

**The AI line.** Predictive-maintenance tools generate more field calls, not fewer; nothing
digital fixes a pump. The reader can run the tools better than the incumbents.

**The kill fact.** No firm to buy, and no engineer to hire, because the skill is personal and
the sector's shortage is exactly the engineers the reader would need. UWV's shortage lists and
one week of calling repair firms answer it.

**The number to settle first.** Wage and availability of an industrial maintenance engineer in
the reader's region, which decides whether the build route exists at all.

**Verdict under the four assumptions.** Ranked fourth. Hands-on, and it fits beside a job only at
the subcontractor stage. Capital moderate: a van, tools and one salary before revenue. No
retraining, but real learning on the floor. If the reader wants to stay out of factories, this
fails together with pitch 2.
