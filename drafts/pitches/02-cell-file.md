# Pitch 2 — The cell file (Family A)

**The unit and the file.** For one robot cell, line or vehicle fleet assembled on a factory floor:
the risk assessment, the validation measurements, the technical file and the CE declaration,
delivered per cell at €1,500–3,000 (estimate) against the incumbent's verified €6,000 per simple
machine and €30,000 per assembly (assembled-machines memo). Second line, same tools: the
substantial-modification file for a retrofitted machine.

**The rule and the date.** Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027
(verified); whoever assembles the cell is the manufacturer of a new machine. EN ISO 10218-2:2025
is published (verified). The new Product Liability Directive applies from 9 December 2026
(verified), which is what makes the modification file sellable to workshop owners.

**Who buys.** Metal, packaging and food firms of ten to a hundred staff that buy a cobot for
machine tending, palletising or welding and install it themselves or through a small integrator;
and Polish- and Ukrainian-owned workshops that retrofit machines. Cobot dealers need the file to
close sales without a full integrator. The incumbents are TÜV-class bodies and the large
integrators, priced above this segment.

**Why you.** Regulatory literacy: the Machinery Regulation is book 18's ground. Engineering habit:
measurements, documents, the evidence trail. Technical managers in Dutch industry work in English
on this subject; the workshop owners speak Polish. No Dutch exam; the safety courses exist in
English (estimate €3–5k, TÜV or Pilz). Tools: a force-pressure measurement kit, €5–10k (estimate).

**The precedent.** Cobots and Machinery Safety Ltd, Corby, one engineer, incorporated 10 July 2019,
active (Companies House, verified); Machine Safety Specialists, Ohio, since 1977 (verified). For
the modification line: the Polish "wymagania minimalne" trade, eight firms surfaced.

**The ninety-day test.** Three pilot cell files at cost through one cobot dealer, on cells already
installed. Under €5,000 including the course and a rented measurement kit.

**Steps two and three.** Step two: a Dutch-speaking safety engineer on the sales side after ten
paid cells. Step three, confirmed by the review as a step and not an entry: one-application
palletising cells installed and signed (Olympus Technologies, Huddersfield, £65–115k per cell),
or buying a small panel builder, which is pitch 4's logic with a product attached.

**The AI line.** Every cheaper robot is another cell, and every cell is a file and a site visit.
The regulation adds cybersecurity clauses that need reading, not software.

**The kill fact.** A dealer or safety body already sells the cell file to SMEs under €3,000, or
Dutch SME technical staff will not buy it in English. The review found dealers doing risk
analysis in-house (Olmia) and one SME using an external expert (Automise, 2021), so the market
exists; the price is the open question. Call two dealers.

**The number to settle first.** How many cobots are installed in the Netherlands per year, and
what share go in without an integrator. Trade body and dealer figures; not yet fetched.

**Verdict under the four assumptions.** Ranked second, and first on fit: the edge is exact, the
unit is countable, the date is real. Hands-on, beside a job for the first ten cells, capital
under €20k, courses of days. If the reader will not travel to factory floors, this fails.
