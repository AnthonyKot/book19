# The pitch step — frame and template (2026-09-05)

## The thesis the pitches are written under

Both Fable research runs on 2026-09-04 reached the same sentence from opposite directions: the AI
build-out "found demand, not an edge"; the survival tables said "the edge is the crew and the
paperwork, not the sector." The ideation metaprompt then reproduced the editor's own best idea
from an abstract seed, which means the eight-part shape is the book's real selection rule.

So the reader has **one edge**, not a list of them: running Polish-, Ukrainian- and
Russian-speaking crews or suppliers behind a Dutch-grade compliance file, read straight from the
EU and Dutch rule before the market prices it. That edge has **two families of use**:

- **Family A — regulation-created work.** A dated rule creates a file that must exist before a
  physical unit can be used, sold, occupied or shipped. The reader sells the file per unit and
  grows into the operator. (Housing/agency compliance, the cell file, the joinery dossier, border
  files, used-equipment inspection, the exposure files.)
- **Family B — businesses that do not die.** The same capital and process discipline applied to
  a small physical firm the CBS survival tables say lasts, bought with the Dutch customer-owner or
  certificate-holder retained. (Owner-independent acquisition, machine repair, heat-pump company,
  certified-inspection microfirms, fleet desk, funeral desk.)

## Assumptions about the four open constraints (the author has not yet answered)

Every pitch is ranked under these; where a different answer changes the ranking the pitch says so.

1. Hands-on, not passive: the reader will run the thing for the first two years.
2. Alongside the job at first: the first paid test and the first ten units fit around a day job;
   replacing the job is a step-two decision.
3. Capital ceiling €100k, of which no more than €50k at risk in year one.
4. No multi-year retraining: courses of days or weeks, in English, are fine; a two-year Dutch trade
   qualification is not.

## Template (one pitch ≈ 400–550 words)

1. **The unit and the file** — what is sold, per what, at what price (verified / estimate).
2. **The rule and the date** — the obligation, its date, its source.
3. **Who buys** — the buyer in one sentence, and why the incumbents price above them.
4. **Why you** — which edge does the work, and the Dutch-language gate answered.
5. **The precedent** — the named firm elsewhere, with its trace.
6. **The ninety-day test** — under €5,000, alongside the job.
7. **Steps two and three** — the partner or hire; the operator or the acquisition.
8. **The AI line** — the mechanism by which AI adds units or leaves the site visit alone.
9. **The kill fact** — one, checkable, and who to call to check it.
10. **The number to settle first** — the single figure a research run must pin before money moves.
11. **Verdict under the four assumptions**, and what changes if an assumption is wrong.

Pitches are written by the coordinating session from the triage notes and the memos; figures carry
their verified/estimate marks from the memos; nothing is added from memory without the mark.
