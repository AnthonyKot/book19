# Pitch 7 — The border files for CEE imports (Family A)

**The unit and the file.** Two files, one desk. The carbon-border file: for one Dutch SME
importer of steel, aluminium or fertiliser from Ukraine, Turkey or Kazakhstan, the CBAM
declarant registration, the installation-level emissions data fetched from the mill, and the
quarterly or annual declaration (estimate: €2,500–6,000 per importer per year). The wood file:
for one importer of Ukrainian pallets, pellets or timber, the deforestation due-diligence
statement with plot geolocation, fetched from Ukrainian forestry offices (estimate: €300–800
per shipment or a retainer).

**The rule and the date.** CBAM: 50-tonne threshold verified at the Dutch emissions authority;
the definitive regime from 1 January 2026, the registration date already passed (verified). EUDR:
30 December 2026 for large operators, 30 June 2027 for small (verified). Both dates have slipped
before; that is the pitch's political risk.

**Who buys.** Dutch SMEs importing two truckloads or more of steel or fertiliser a year, and
pallet and biomass traders buying Ukrainian wood; too small for the big-four consultancies, too
exposed to skip the file. The customs brokers are the incumbents and the kill fact.

**Why you.** The data sits in a mill or a forestry office that answers in Russian or Ukrainian;
the rule is read from the source. No Dutch exam; the customer is an importer who works in
English. This is the strongest language edge in the whole pool.

**The precedent.** Ingdilligenz GmbH, Würzburg, founded 2021, twenty-plus consultants, sells the
CBAM-representative role (verified, own site plus trace); RECO, Gliwice, an EUDR desk for SME
importers (KRS-registered, verified).

**The ninety-day test.** One paid data pack for one importer of Ukrainian steel: get the mill's
emissions data into the declarant's template. Under €500 in costs.

**Steps two and three.** Step two: a retainer book of ten importers and a Ukrainian-speaking
associate in Kyiv or Lviv. Step three: the importer of record itself, holding the goods, which
is pitch 11's shape applied to steel and wood.

**The AI line.** The files are data work; AI makes them cheaper to assemble, which cuts the price
but not the demand, and the demand is a legal obligation per shipment. Honest flag: this is the
least physical pitch in the book. It passes the "outside software" line and sits at the edge of
"physical".

**The kill fact.** The importers' customs brokers bundle the declaration for free, or the
deadlines slip again. Call two brokers; read the next Commission omnibus.

**The number to settle first.** How many Dutch importers above 50 tonnes registered before the
deadline, and how many did not. The emissions authority and customs hold both.

**Verdict under the four assumptions.** Ranked seventh: beside a job, capital under €5k, no
retraining, hands-on at a desk. If the reader wants site work rather than desk work, this drops;
if passive income is the goal, this is the one pitch that can become a retainer book run by two
people.
