# Triage of the ideation pass — Fable 5.1, from knowledge and judgment (2026-09-04, evening)

Pool: two Fable 5.1 ideation agents run on `scripts/prompts/idea-metaprompt.md` with two abstract
seeds ("assembled machines": the moment a bought thing becomes something someone must sign for;
"crossing flows": the moment a border turns a shipment or a person into paperwork), eight kept ideas
between them; then two review agents applying the precedent rule (a named small firm elsewhere that
already lives on the shape) and verifying what the ideation runs had marked unverified. The test of the
method: could an agent reach the cobot CE service the author's editor had proposed, without being told?
It did, in the first seed, priced against a verified Dutch incumbent (€6,000 per simple machine,
€30,000 per assembly), and the review found a one-engineer firm in Corby that has lived on exactly
this since 2019. The shape holds. The second seed went somewhere the editor had not: border files.

## Added to the kept list

- **NEW #2. The cell file: CE for robot cells, lines and fleets assembled on SME floors.** From
  20 January 2027 (verified) the Machinery Regulation makes whoever assembles the cell the
  manufacturer of a new machine; the new EN ISO 10218-2:2025 is published. Per cell: risk
  assessment, validation measurements, technical file, declaration, at €1,500–3,000 against the
  incumbent's €6,000. Precedents: Cobots and Machinery Safety Ltd (Corby, one engineer, 2019) and
  Machine Safety Specialists (Ohio, 1977). Second product line from the same run: the
  substantial-modification file for retrofitted machines in Polish- and Ukrainian-owned workshops
  (new Product Liability Directive, 9 December 2026, verified; Polish "wymagania minimalne" firms
  as precedent). Step three, confirmed as step three and not entry: one-application palletising
  cells, installed and signed (Olympus Technologies, Huddersfield, £65–115k per cell). Ranked
  second because it is the best fit of edge to unit in the book: regulatory literacy and
  engineering habit, English-speaking technical buyers, no Dutch exam, tools under €10k.
- **NEW #7. Border files for CEE imports: the carbon file and the wood file.** Two ideas, one desk.
  Dutch SME importers of Ukrainian, Turkish and Kazakh steel, aluminium and fertiliser need CBAM
  declarant status and verified installation emissions (50-tonne threshold verified at the Dutch
  emissions authority; the registration date has already passed), and the data sits in a mill that
  answers in Russian or Ukrainian. Importers of Ukrainian pallets, pellets and timber need the
  deforestation due-diligence statement with plot geolocation from 30 December 2026 (verified),
  and the polygons sit in Ukrainian forestry offices. Precedents: Ingdilligenz GmbH (Würzburg, 2021,
  sells the CBAM-representative role) and RECO (Gliwice, EUDR desk for SME importers). Honest
  flag: this is desk work attached to a truckload, not a site visit. It passes the book line
  (no software product) but sits at the edge of "physical". Kept because the language edge is the
  strongest in the pool and the deadlines are real.
- **NEW #8. Used-equipment inspection and the not-waste file for CEE buyers.** Dutch machinery,
  workshop kit and dismantled greenhouse equipment sold to Poland and Ukraine needs, from 21 May
  2026 (verified), a per-lot functionality record to travel as goods rather than waste, and the
  buyer in Poland or Ukraine wants an inspection report in their language before paying. Mevas
  (Germany, 2006, 700+ inspections a year, from €720 a machine) proves the per-unit economics with
  the payer on the buyer's side. The used-greenhouse-equipment line the author raised sits here.
  Physical, per unit, language edge, sanctions line drawn at Russia and Belarus.

## What the pass says about the method

The metaprompt reproduces the editor's best idea from an abstract seed, which means the shape (iron
plus paper, dated rule, small English-speaking buyers, no Dutch exam, per-unit price, AI adds units,
one kill fact, one precedent) is the book's actual selection rule and can be stated as such in the
method chapter. The precedent rule did the most work: it sorted eight ideas into three tiers in one
pass and cut the one that sounded best on paper. Cost: four agents, roughly 400k tokens.
