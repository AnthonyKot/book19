# Crowdfunding research — Gemini 3.8 Flash run, summary (2026-09-03, written by the coordinating session)

Gemini's run is in and its numbers verified against the downloaded Kickstarter dataset (JetKVM,
eufyMake, Snapmaker U1, RingConn, BB-777, Smith Blade, Makera Z1, Flux Keyboard all match row for
row, non-USD amounts correctly converted). Full output: `drafts/reviews/crowdfunding-findings-agy.md`
and `drafts/reviews/agy/`.

**The gap is real but narrow.** Of 1.4 billion dollars pledged to physical products on Kickstarter
since 2023, 88% came from outside the EU, but the top tier is Shenzhen firms that already have a
German GmbH or Dutch B.V. and are on Amazon within two months. What's left is smaller foreign makers
stalled by EU rules. Gemini's phrase for the resulting business: a sniper, not a wholesaler.

**Its four candidates, all with no EU seller found:**
1. JetKVM, a remote-KVM device: 5.9 million dollars, 45,880 backers, US. Needs the Cyber Resilience
   Act, radio equipment and WEEE work. Kill fact: cheap Pi-KVM clones.
2. Willcook packable microwave bag, Japan-only, 2,450 backers. Food-contact and battery rules. Kill
   fact: a 20-euro lunchbox from Temu.
3. iMini F1 helmet intercom, Taiwan, 4,560 backers. Radio, CRA and the helmet-safety standard, which
   may void approval.
4. Bumpboxx BB-777 boombox, 7.1 million dollars, US-only shipping. Electrical, battery and WEEE.
   Kill fact: cassette-mechanism returns.

Plus a top-40 table of proven products with an EU-seller check for each, which is the useful
artefact to scan.

**The pattern flagged:** three of the four are connected electronics, which is exactly where the
Cyber Resilience Act from September 2026 makes an EU importer valuable and liable at once. That is
book 18's essay 1 knowledge turned into a distribution business. Codex's list follows; the
comparison goes in `drafts/notes/2026-09-03-comparison.md`.
