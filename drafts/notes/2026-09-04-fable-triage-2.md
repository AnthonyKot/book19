# Triage of the two Fable research runs — Fable 5.1, from knowledge and judgment (2026-09-04)

Pool: ten candidates from two runs the earlier instruments did not cover, both run by Fable 5.1 agents
on 2026-09-04: the physical footprint of the AI build-out (five candidates, 29 saved excerpts) and the
boring-business lens on CBS survival, margin and exit data (five candidates, 24 saved excerpts, raw
CBS OData rows saved beside the computed figures). Spot-checked figures trace. What follows is my
judgment on whether each *business* stands up, against the same edge inventory and gates as the
first triage (`2026-09-03-fable-triage.md`).

## Added to the kept list

- **NEW #3. Buy a machine-repair or industrial-maintenance microfirm (SBI 33).** The quietest
  survivor in the whole CBS series: 79% of 2018 starters alive in 2023, the lowest small-firm exit
  rate of any sector tested, 17 bankruptcies on 14,175 firms, 39% of industrial employers aged 55+.
  It is the one sector where the reader's engineering habit is an edge without the business becoming
  IT, and industrial customers work in English. Two honest weaknesses: no broker category exists, so
  the pool is found by walking industrial estates and asking accountants, not by browsing; and the
  skill may sit in the seller alone. First test as proposed: one paid maintenance-audit-plus-spares
  job for an English-speaking plant, sold through an existing repair firm. Kill fact: no firm to buy.
  Pushes the heat-pump company to #4 and the rest down one.
- **NEW #9. B2B fleet-service desk for CEE-owned transport and construction firms.** The demand
  angle is the reader's own: 36% of transport owners and 31% of construction owners in the
  Netherlands are foreign-born, and their vans need inspection, tachograph and maintenance in Polish.
  Not a garage to buy first; a desk that sells prepaid fleet-service vouchers through Polish agencies
  and fulfils at a partner garage. Capital-light, testable in a month. Kill facts: the APK inspector
  licence is a Dutch exam (unverified by the run), so the garage stays a partner; and if the partner
  garage's income is vehicle trade rather than repair, the margin story goes.

## Held, pending one fact

- **Grid civil-works crew for the grid operators' contractors.** The demand is the strongest dated
  number any instrument has produced: 15,014 firms on the connection waiting list, 2,183 transformer
  stations built in 2025, a 28,000-technician deficit to 2029, 23,000 of it at contractors. The
  reader's edge is the same as in #1 and #5: recruiting Polish and Ukrainian crews and running the
  compliance dossier (VCA, hours, the Wtta line between contracting and labour supply). But every
  crew member needs a grid-operator safety designation (BEI/VIAG), and the run reports the exams as
  Dutch-only. If a contractor can train and designate crews in Polish or English, this becomes the
  step up from #1; if not, it is a labour-supply business the reader cannot staff. One phone call
  to a qualified contractor settles it. Held, not ranked.

## Merged into an existing candidate

- **Acquire a SCIOS generator-inspection microfirm** → into #6 (asbestos survey), which becomes a
  class: *buy a certified-inspection microbusiness and keep the certificate-holder*. The AI build-out
  adds the generator fleets as a customer; the deal logic is identical. Pest control (per-person
  biocide certificate, the one listing found at roughly €240k revenue and €78k EBITDA, inside the
  €100k-plus-Qredits envelope) joins the same class. Three instances, one chapter.
- **Specialised-cleaning contractor** → into #2 (owner-independent B2B physical business) as a
  worked example with its warning attached: the best margin of the physical services tested, and
  the worst churn, with wages at 53% of revenue. Fine to buy if the debt service survives one
  collective-agreement rise; the run's own kill fact.

## Cut, and why

- **Behind-the-meter battery and flex-contract packages.** The grid operators and the regulator give
  queued firms the flex products directly, the Dutch energy-consultancy market for congestion is
  already crowded, and what is left is engineering consultancy: book 18's world, not this book's.
- **Wpbr-licensed guarding firm for data-centre and substation sites.** The licence is cheap and has
  no exam, but every guard needs the Dutch security diploma, guarding margins are thin, and the
  hyperscalers buy from audited national firms. A labour business with the language gate pointed
  the wrong way.
- **ITAD collection and refurbished-server channel to CEE.** A real trade, but hyperscaler and
  large-colocation kit is locked in global disposal contracts, the business is hardware trading
  (parked with #8, the importer of record, as IT-adjacent), and the Russian-speaking resale network
  the run counted as an edge is a sanctions problem: servers are dual-use goods and cannot go to
  Russia or Belarus. Cut.
- **Small road haulage with a hired transport manager.** The run tested it and said do not enter:
  margins fell to 6.8%, exits and bankruptcies are high, and the exams exclude English. Agreed.

## What the two runs say together

Both honest paragraphs reach the same sentence from opposite directions. The AI-footprint run:
"this instrument found demand, not an edge." The boring-business run: "the edge is the crew and the
paperwork, not the sector." That is the book's thesis in one line, and the earlier triage had it only
implicitly. The reader's advantage is a single capability, running Polish-, Ukrainian- and
Russian-speaking crews or suppliers behind a Dutch-grade compliance dossier, and the survival tables
choose the sector for it. Candidates #1, #5 and the held grid crew are that capability applied to
housing, joinery and the grid; #2, #3 and #6 are the same capital applied to businesses that the
data says do not die. The pitch step should be written that way: one edge, two families of use.
