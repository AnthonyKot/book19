SOURCE S5 — entrepreneurs by origin
Publisher: Statistics Netherlands (CBS)
URL: https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/nieuws/2024/32/bijna-1-miljoen-ondernemers-erbij-sinds-2010
Fetch date: 2026-09-03

Excerpt:
In 2023 nearly 400,000 entrepreneurs in the Netherlands were born abroad; one third
had an EU origin. Poland was the largest individual country with 35,000 entrepreneurs,
and slightly more than half of those Polish entrepreneurs worked in construction.
There were 2,110 first-generation Ukrainian starters in 2023, versus 120 in 2010.

Limit: entrepreneurs are not the same as establishments, active service providers or
language-matched providers. The article supplies national, not municipal, counts.


SOURCE S6 — KVK trend in foreign-born entrepreneurship
Publisher: Netherlands Chamber of Commerce (KVK)
URL: https://regiodata.kvk.nl/news/blijvende-groei-aantal-bedrijven-in-2022/89
Fetch date: 2026-09-03

Excerpt:
The share of entrepreneurs born outside the Netherlands rose from 13% in 2013 to
18% in 2023. Poland was the largest foreign-born entrepreneur group. Compared with
the preceding period, starters from Ukraine and the former Soviet Union increased by
more than 218% and almost 44%, respectively. KVK notes that Ukrainians must have the
appropriate permissions.

Limit: “former Soviet Union” is not synonymous with Russian-speaking, and growth
rates do not show the stock or demand for any service category.


SOURCE S7 — Ukrainian refugees and self-employment
Publisher: Netherlands Chamber of Commerce (KVK)
URL: https://www.kvk.nl/starten/oekraiense-vluchteling-inschrijven-bij-kvk/
Fetch date: 2026-09-03

Excerpt:
People protected under the Ukrainian Temporary Protection Directive may work as
employees without a TWV. A customer who hires such a person as a self-employed
worker needs a TWV. KVK says applications for common assignments in hospitality,
construction, personal services and agriculture are rejected because sufficient labour
is available in the Netherlands or EEA.

Limit: updated April 2026; it concerns refugees under temporary protection, not all
Ukrainian nationals or incorporated businesses.


SOURCE S8 — Roemer advisory report (2020)
Publisher: Aanjaagteam Bescherming Arbeidsmigranten / Dutch central government
Landing page: https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/2020/10/30/tweede-advies-aanjaagteam-bescherming-arbeidsmigranten-1
PDF URL: https://www.fnv.nl/getmedia/8b4019be-67f1-4979-a07b-a5a0e385cf7b/Rapport-Roemer.pdf
Fetch date: 2026-09-03

Excerpt:
The final report describes a worker’s dependence on the employer/agency across work,
housing, transport and health insurance. Identified risks include underpayment, poor
housing, unsafe work and the simultaneous loss of job, housing and insurance. It makes
50 recommendations around regulation, registration, housing, health, enforcement and
access to information.

Limit: a national policy diagnosis from 2020, not a present-day willingness-to-pay
study and not specific to Polish, Ukrainian, Russian or Belarusian speakers.
