Pitch 1 — The housing and agency file (Family A)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/pitches/01-housing-agency-compliance.md.

The unit and the file. A pre-audit of one labour-migrant housing location against the SNF standard, delivered as a dated report with the fixes listed and the evidence photographed, at €500 per location (estimate), sold in threes; then the agency's admission dossier for the Wtta regime as a fixed-fee package (estimate €2,500–4,000). Recurring: annual re-audit, rule-change updates.

The rule and the date. The SNF housing standard tightens on 1 October 2026 (verified, regulation memo); the Wtta admission regime for temporary-work agencies is expected in 2027 (verified as expected, not yet in force); Hollands Kroon's local housing rules, in force 17 April 2026, add a register duty and an 80-person cap per site (verified, AI-footprint memo).

Who buys. Polish-owned and Dutch-owned agencies and housing operators with roughly 20 to 200 placed workers, who cannot afford a compliance officer and are priced out by the six accredited SNF inspectors and the large HR-law firms. About 20,000 registered locations and 700 registered enterprises (regulation memo).

Why you. The tenants speak Polish, Ukrainian and Russian; the audit is half interview. The rule is read from the source, in English and Dutch. No Dutch exam exists for this work; the customer contact is with owners who are often Polish. The Dutch-language gate is the municipal and SNF paperwork, which a Dutch-speaking partner handles from step two.

The precedent. No single firm was fetched for this shape; the SNF inspectors and HR-law firms are the incumbents, and the pre-audit market sits below them. This is the one pitch whose precedent is the incumbents' price, not a small firm. A research run should find one.

The ninety-day test. A €1,500 mock audit for three locations of one Polish-owned agency, sold through the reader's network, delivered in one weekend. Under €500 in costs.

Steps two and three. Step two: a Dutch-speaking partner for municipal contact and the agencies' Wtta filings. Step three: the operator, running arrivals, onboarding, housing registers and audits as a service for ten agencies, or acquiring a small agency and being the compliant one.

The AI line. AI does not inspect a bunk bed or interview a tenant; it makes the reporting cheaper, which widens the margin, and the rules keep adding fields.

The kill fact. The SNF reserves pre-audit advice to its accredited inspectors, or the agencies use the free public information points and will not pay. One call to SNF and to two agencies.

The number to settle first. What an accredited SNF inspection costs an operator today, and how many locations fail first time. SNF publishes the first; the second is a question for an inspector.

Verdict under the four assumptions. Ranked first: hands-on, fits beside a job, capital under €5k, no retraining. If the reader wants passive income, this drops to the middle: it is consulting until a team runs it. If the capital ceiling is higher, step three arrives sooner.
