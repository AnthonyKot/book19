Ideation — certified object (seed D) — Fable 5.1, 2026-09-04

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/ideation/certified-object/ideas.md.

Seed: what a building, dwelling, vehicle, vessel, installation or piece of equipment in the Netherlands must carry a certificate, label, inspection or registration for before it can be sold, let, insured, connected, put on the road or water, or kept in use; the moment an owned thing becomes unsellable or uninsurable without a piece of paper, and who is short of the people who issue it. Worked from knowledge first; verification budget twenty-five fetches, spent only on precedents, kill facts and the one number each kept idea depends on. Excerpts under sources/. Off the table: everything kept, held, watched or cut in the three triage notes and the two earlier ideation folders (assembled-machines, crossing-flows). Nothing below renames those.

A note on the seed before the list. Most of what this seed names is gated by a Dutch-language personal certificate (EP-adviser, SCIOS, asbestos DIA, APK, BRL 6010, CO-certification, TCVT, CCV) or by designated bodies that no small firm can become (lifts, pressure equipment, playgrounds). The book has already handled the buyable ones as the certified-inspection class (#9). What is left, and what this run kept, are the three shortages that no exam guards: electricians, structural engineers and energy auditors, each attached to a dated rule.

3. Kept ideas (three)

A. The car-park and depot charging file

  1. Iron plus paper. Chargers on an SME car park or van depot cannot be connected or used without a grid application (a non-firm contract in congested areas), a load-management design that fits the chargers behind the existing connection, a NEN 1010 commissioning report and the Bbl compliance record; DC units of 50 kW and more add the AFIR card-payment retrofit.
  2. Dated rule. EPBD recast 2024/1275 Art. 14: all non-residential buildings with more than 20 parking spaces by 1 January 2027 (verified); the one-per-ten ratio and the transposition text are unverified; Dutch Bbl today: one point since 1 January 2025 (verified, art. 3.87b–c); vans out of the zero-emission zones from 1 January 2028 (from memory).
  3. Buyers. Owner-occupied halls, workshops and depots of 10–100 staff, CEE-owned transport and construction firms first; incumbents are Dutch installers and charge-point operators who sell the operator contract, not the file.
  4. Edge. Reads the Bbl transposition and the grid code; measures the site's spare capacity and designs load management from it; Polish electricians (NEN 3140 designation courses exist in Polish). No exam.
  5. Per unit. €1,500–3,000 per site file (estimate); first test three sites through one Polish agency's clients, about €4,500; capital under €10k. Step two: a Dutch installer with grid-operator standing. Step three: own and operate the chargers, back-end outsourced.
  6. AI. AI loads deepen grid congestion, so every car park needs a measured, load-managed design instead of a catalogue order, and the measurement is a visit.
  7. Kill fact. The Dutch transposition exempts buildings owned and occupied by SMEs, as Germany's GEIG does (verified for Germany, unverified for the EU text and for the Bbl): then the owner-occupier owes nothing and the landlord market is Dutch and large.
  8. Precedent. AMP Renewables Ltd, Washington (UK), Ltd since 2015, trading since 1985, NICEIC contractor: workplace and fleet charging with the DNO/G99 application, load balancing and the OZEV grant file, for "small offices (2–5 chargers)" up to depots, at £1,000–2,500 per charger installed (verified, own site plus Companies House 09420963). Caveat: a general contractor, and UK demand is grant-assisted.

B. The plant energy file

  1. Iron plus paper. A plant, depot or cold store kept in use owes an energy audit above 10 TJ a year, all carriers counted (a haulier with roughly ten trucks is in, on diesel alone: estimate), the four-yearly energy-saving report to the omgevingsdienst, and building automation above 290 kW. The audit is a walk through the plant with a clamp meter and a sub-metering plan.
  2. Dated rule. EED 2023/1791 Art. 11: first audit by 11 October 2026, certified management system above 85 TJ by 11 October 2027 (both verified). The Netherlands had not implemented the 10 TJ test at fetch date (verified, RVO), so the Dutch deadline is late and its form open. Informatieplicht round 1 December 2027 and BACS above 290 kW since 1 January 2026: from memory.
  3. Buyers. CEE-owned food plants, metal shops, hauliers and cold stores of 10–100 staff; incumbents are Dutch energy consultancies priced for the large-enterprise audit of the old rule.
  4. Edge. No personal certificate is stated for EED auditors in NL (RVO page, verified by absence); reads the directive and the Erkende Maatregelenlijst; builds the evidence trail; the buyers' language.
  5. Per unit. €3,000–8,000 per site audit, €500–1,500 per energy-saving report (estimates); first test one Polish-owned plant, about €4,000; capital under €5k. Step two: a Dutch partner for the omgevingsdienst. Step three: install the measures (metering, drives, heat recovery) or buy a small installer.
  6. AI. Automation adds kilowatts to the same plant and electrification pushes more sites over 10 TJ, and each new site is a visit, not a dataset.
  7. Kill fact. The Dutch implementation lets the existing onderzoeksplicht or informatieplicht report satisfy Article 11, so no firm between 10 TJ and the old threshold buys anything new.
  8. Precedent. DeltaUWB GmbH (Ingenieurbüro Schwark), Blankenhain, Thuringia: founded 2016, GmbH since 2022, second office in Gotha, a family team of engineers and master craftsmen selling DIN EN 16247 audits to "producing Mittelstand" (metal, textile, bakeries) and municipalities (verified, own site plus two directory listings; BAFA list not fetched). Caveat: German SME audits are BAFA-subsidised; the Dutch buyer pays in full.

C. The roof file

  1. Iron plus paper. PV on an SME roof needs a structural roof-load calculation, the insurer's fire layout and Scope 12 pre-check, a grid application or zero-feed-in setting, and a self-consumption design once net metering ends. The Netherlands has no protected structural-engineer title, so a Polish engineer's calculation stands (from knowledge).
  2. Dated rule. Net metering ends for households and small businesses on 1 January 2027 (verified, Eerste Kamer, 17 December 2024). EPBD Art. 10: solar on all new public and non-residential buildings above 250 m² by 31 December 2026 (verified), further deadlines 2027 and 2029 (categories from memory).
  3. Buyers. Owner-occupier SMEs putting PV on halls, and mid-size installers who subcontract the structural check; incumbents are Dutch engineering firms and the installers' bundled quotes.
  4. Edge. Engineering habit; Polish structural engineers and PV crews as supply; reads the grid code and the insurers' conditions. No exam.
  5. Per unit. €1,500–3,000 per roof file (estimate; the German precedent charges from €1,500); first test three roofs, about €4,500; capital under €10k. Step two: a Dutch installer partner. Step three: rooftop operator, owning the array and selling the power.
  6. AI. Congestion from AI loads makes the self-consumption design the deciding document, and the roof survey is a person on a roof.
  7. Kill fact. Dutch installers include the structural check and the Scope 12 pre-check in every B2B quote at no separate price. Unchecked; likely partly true.
  8. Precedent. Gutachten Graf GmbH, Lohmar (NRW): a TÜV-certified PV expert selling acceptance inspections, structural and technical checks, yield analysis and construction supervision to commercial roof owners, "starting €1,500", industrial projects "€3,000–5,000+", supervision about 2% of the installation (verified, own site plus Northdata register extract; founding year not found). Caveat: the owner also runs an installer, GRAF.SOLAR GmbH.

5. Honest paragraph

This seed found nothing a Dutch founder with the same reading could not: every rule here is printed in Dutch, and Dutch installers, engineers and advisers already sell each file. What it found is narrower: the three shortages the seed points at that no Dutch exam guards (electricians, structural engineers, energy auditors), each with a 2026–2027 date, and buyers the reader can phone in their own language. Vessels and vehicles gave nothing in-seed. My money would go to B, the plant energy file: the EU date is five weeks away, the Netherlands is late, no certificate stands in the way, and CEE-owned hauliers and plants are the reader's own network.
