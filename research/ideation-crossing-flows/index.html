Ideation — crossing flows (agent B, Fable 5.1, 2026-09-04)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/ideation/crossing-flows/ideas.md.

Seed: physical things and people moving between the Netherlands and Poland, Ukraine and the wider Russian-speaking world, in either direction, that need a file to be legal on arrival. Method as in the brief: knowledge first, then at most ten fetches to check kill facts and one load-bearing number per idea. Off the table (kept, held or cut in the two triages): housing/agency compliance, joinery/steel agency, CRA importer, fleet desk, funeral desk, ITAD-to-CEE, haulage, textile waste, grid crew, heat pumps, asbestos and the certified-inspection class. Nothing below renames those.

Screening note. R1–R4 (and R5, cut for overlap) share one mechanism: since 2022 the goods that used to cross the eastern border with an invoice now cross with a file the EU invented (deforestation, carbon, sanctions, waste, construction performance), and the file is only obtainable by someone who can read the EU rule and talk to the Ukrainian or Central Asian counterparty in its own language and its own registers. That is the seed's whole finding.

2. Kept ideas (four)

Verification budget: ten fetches spent; four returned usable text (EUDR, CBAM hub, WSR, sanctions hub), six failed (EUR-Lex three times by two methods, two Commission sub-pages 404). Everything marked "unverified" below is from memory and must be settled by the research run.

K1. The deforestation file for Ukrainian wood entering the Netherlands

  1. Iron plus paper. A truckload of Ukrainian pallets, pellets, timber or furniture cannot be placed on the Dutch market without a due-diligence statement in the EU Information System, backed by the geolocation of every forest plot and the harvest's legality papers (scope verified).
  2. Dated rule. Regulation 2023/1115 applies from 30 December 2026 (large and medium operators) and 30 June 2027 (micro and small), verified.
  3. Buyers. Dutch pallet makers, pellet traders, timber and furniture importers of 10–100 staff buying from Ukrainian sawmills. Incumbents: certification bodies and EUDR software priced for multinationals.
  4. Edge. Reads the regulation and Ukraine's electronic timber-accounting system in Ukrainian; gets plot polygons from a state forest enterprise by phone; makes the evidence trail a protocol. No exam.
  5. Price. Per supplier onboarding and per statement. Test: one pallet importer, one sawmill, three shipments, about €3,000. Step two: a Dutch partner who owns the importers. Step three: importer of record for Ukrainian timber.
  6. AI. Satellite checks raise the number of statements challenged; the polygons and permits still come from a forestry office in Zhytomyr, and reconstruction trade adds trucks.
  7. Kill fact. Ukraine's state forestry company already issues EUDR-ready geolocation packs with every export contract, free, so the forwarder files the statement.

K2. The carbon file for non-EU steel, aluminium and fertiliser imported by Dutch SMEs

  1. Iron plus paper. A lot of Ukrainian, Turkish, Kazakh or Georgian steel, aluminium or fertiliser cannot be imported from 1 January 2026 unless the importer is an authorised CBAM declarant, and each year's imports need a verified declaration of embedded emissions per producing installation, (regime and sectors verified; mechanics from memory).
  2. Dated rule. Definitive regime 1 January 2026 (verified); first certificate sales and annual declaration in 2027 and a proposed extension to downstream goods (both unverified).
  3. Buyers. Steel fabricators, trailer builders, façade firms and fertiliser blenders of 10–100 staff buying outside the EU. Incumbents: customs brokers for large accounts, Big-4 desks, SaaS.
  4. Edge. The hard part is getting actual emissions data out of a mill in Zaporizhzhia, Iskenderun or Temirtau, whose energy engineer speaks Russian or Ukrainian. Automates calculation and evidence trail; the Dutch authority (NEa) works in English.
  5. Price. Per installation data pack and per annual declaration. Test: one fabricator, one supplier, one quarter, about €4,000. Step two: a Dutch customs broker. Step three: authorised declarant reselling CBAM-cleared steel.
  6. AI. Calculation is automated; the demand is a person who extracts a verified dataset from a non-EU mill, and the scope widens.
  7. Kill fact. The de minimis threshold (50 tonnes a year, unverified) removes nearly every 10–100-staff importer, or Ukraine is granted a derogation.

K3. The sanctions and dual-use file for Dutch machinery going to Central Asia and the Caucasus

  1. Iron plus paper. A used CNC machine, pump, tractor or parts order cannot leave the Netherlands for Central Asia, the Caucasus, Turkey or the UAE without a dual-use classification, a Common High Priority screening, a "no re-export to Russia" clause, an end-user due-diligence file and often a CDIU licence.
  2. Dated rule. Article 12g mandatory since March 2024, end-user due diligence since late 2024 (both unverified); 21st package adopted 23 July 2026 (verified). A running clock through 2028.
  3. Buyers. Dutch pump, machinery and agri-tech firms of 10–100 staff with Central Asian customers. Incumbents: law firms at €300 an hour; the CDIU is free but slow.
  4. Edge. Reads the Regulation; verifies the Kazakh or Uzbek buyer in Russian (registry, site call, photos, a visit), the network working on the enforcement side. No exam.
  5. Price. Per order file; per end-user verification. Test: three shipments for one exporter, about €3,000. Step two: a Dutch customs broker for licences. Step three: buy a small forwarder and become exporter of record.
  6. AI. Screening is a commodity; the duty is a human judgment of end use that customs expect a named person to sign, and packages keep adding items.
  7. Kill fact. A settlement lifts Regulation 833/2014 by 2028, or forwarders already sell the screening and clause for tens of euros a shipment.

K4. The "not waste" file for used equipment sent from the Netherlands to Ukraine and Moldova

  1. Iron plus paper. A used tractor, generator, forklift or machine tool sent from a Dutch yard to Ukraine is either "used goods" or "waste" at the border. Used electrical equipment already needs a per-item functionality record to count as goods (WEEE Annex VI); the new Waste Shipment Regulation makes that question decide legality.
  2. Dated rule. Regulation 2024/1157 applies from 21 May 2026; from 21 May 2027 green-listed waste may not go to non-OECD countries (Ukraine, Moldova, Georgia) unless listed (verified).
  3. Buyers. Dutch used-agri, construction and medical equipment dealers and refurbishers of 10–100 staff shipping east. Incumbents: EVOA consultants at big-firm prices, forwarders who refuse the load.
  4. Edge. Reads the Regulation and the WEEE annex; designs and runs the functionality test protocol (serials, photos, load test, signed record); reads the consignee's papers in Ukrainian. Unlike cut ITAD: no servers, no trading, no Russia.
  5. Price. Per lot. Test: two lots for one agri-equipment dealer, about €2,500. Step two: a Dutch partner for notifications and ILT. Step three: become the refurbisher-exporter.
  6. AI. No model runs a load test on a 2012 tractor in Barneveld; reconstruction adds lots, stricter rules add a file per lot.
  7. Kill fact. Customs and ILT do not in practice stop used-machinery shipments to Ukraine (only WEEE and cars are policed), so nobody pays.

3. Facts a research run must settle

K1 (EUDR wood)

  1. What did the December 2025 amendment change for small operators and for downstream traders relying on an upstream statement? (EUR-Lex, amending regulation; Commission FAQ.)
  2. Value and volume of NL imports of HS 4401 (pellets, firewood), 4407, 4412, 4415 (pallets) and 9403 from Ukraine, 2023–2025. (Eurostat Comext.)
  3. Is Ukraine "standard" or "low" risk in the Commission's country benchmarking, and does that decide simplified due diligence? (Commission benchmarking implementing act.)
  4. Does Ukraine's state forestry company or its timber-accounting system already issue plot geolocation with export contracts? (Ліси України, ЕОД documentation; a call to one sawmill.)
  5. How many Dutch importers of Ukrainian wood products exist and how many staff do they have? (CBS, KvK, trade bodies EPV/NBvT.)
  6. What do certification bodies and EUDR SaaS charge a 30-person importer? (Preferred by Nature, osapiens, price lists or quotes.)
  7. Who is the "operator" when a Ukrainian forwarder delivers DAP to a Dutch yard? (Regulation Article 2 and Commission guidance.)
  8. Can a non-EU manufacturer's Dutch representative file statements on the importer's behalf (authorised representative, Article 6)? (Regulation text.)
  9. First refusals: has NVWA (the Dutch competent authority) announced its enforcement approach and staffing? (NVWA, Rijksoverheid.)
  10. What is the EU Timber Regulation status of the same importers, which decides whether they fall under the 30 December 2026 date? (NVWA registers.)

K2 (CBAM)

  1. Exact de minimis threshold and how it is measured after the 2025 omnibus (Regulation 2025/2083). (EUR-Lex.)
  2. Deadlines: authorised-declarant application grace, first certificate sale, first annual declaration and surrender. (Regulation and Commission implementing acts.)
  3. Is a Dutch importer allowed to appoint an indirect customs representative or a third party to act as declarant? (CBAM Regulation Article 5 and 17; NEa guidance.)
  4. Number of Dutch firms that applied for authorised-declarant status and their size distribution. (NEa, Rijksoverheid.)
  5. NL imports of CBAM goods (HS 72, 73, 76, 31, 2523) from Ukraine, Turkey, Kazakhstan, Georgia, 2024–2025, by count of importers. (Comext; CBS.)
  6. Is a Ukraine derogation under discussion, and what is its state? (Council/Commission, Ukrainian ministry statements.)
  7. What do customs brokers and Big-4 charge for a small importer's annual declaration? (Quotes.)
  8. Are default values still allowed for 2026 imports, and at what mark-up? (Implementing regulation on default values.)
  9. Does the verification of actual emissions require an accredited verifier for each installation, and who accredits for Ukrainian mills? (Implementing regulation on verification; NEa.)
  10. Has the Commission tabled the downstream-products extension and with what date? (Commission proposal, December 2025 or later.)

K3 (sanctions and dual-use)

  1. Exact scope and dates of Articles 12g and 12gb of Regulation 833/2014 as consolidated after the 21st package. (EUR-Lex consolidated text.)
  2. Which HS codes are on the Common High Priority list today and which coincide with Dutch machinery exports? (Commission CHP list; Comext NL exports to KZ, KG, UZ, AM, GE, TR, AE.)
  3. Number of Dutch exporters to those seven destinations with 10–100 staff. (CBS trade-by-enterprise statistics.)
  4. CDIU licence volumes, processing time and refusal rates for dual-use exports to Central Asia. (Rijksoverheid, Dutch customs annual report.)
  5. What forwarders and screening vendors actually charge an SME per shipment. (DKV, Kuehne+Nagel, Descartes quotes.)
  6. Enforcement: Dutch prosecutions or fines against SMEs for circumvention since 2024. (FIOD, OM press releases.)
  7. Does the anti-circumvention tool currently name any Central Asian country? (Council decisions.)
  8. Trend of NL exports to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan in CHP items 2022–2025, the circumvention signal itself. (Comext.)
  9. Professional indemnity: can a sole trader insure this work in NL, and at what premium? (Broker quote.)
  10. Reputational check: would a Dutch SME hire a Belarusian-born adviser for sanctions work? (Three cold conversations; honest answer needed.)

K4 (waste-shipment "not waste" file)

  1. Exact text of the used-goods and functionality provisions in Regulation 2024/1157 and WEEE Directive Annex VI as applied by ILT. (EUR-Lex; ILT guidance on EVOA.)
  2. Has Ukraine or Moldova notified the Commission to receive green-listed waste under the new procedure? (Commission list, when published.)
  3. NL exports of used machinery and vehicles to Ukraine and Moldova 2022–2025 (HS 84, 87 second-hand where identifiable). (Comext; CBS.)
  4. ILT inspection and refusal counts for shipments to Ukraine since 2022. (ILT annual reports, WOB/WOO requests.)
  5. Ukraine's import rules for used machinery: conformity declaration, age limits, duties, the humanitarian-aid route. (Ukrainian customs, Ministry of Economy.)
  6. Who currently prepares notifications and proof-of-function files for Dutch dealers, and at what price? (EVOA consultants; dealer interviews.)
  7. Number of Dutch used-agri and used-construction dealers of 10–100 staff exporting east. (Fedecom, BMWT member lists; KvK.)
  8. Does the Dutch–Ukrainian reconstruction channel (embassies, Rijksdienst, NGOs) run into these files? (RVO, Dutch embassy Kyiv.)
  9. Insurance and liability of the person who signs the functionality record. (Broker quote.)

4. Honest paragraph

This seed found one thing a Dutch founder with the same reading could not: the counterparty. Every kept file is written in Brussels but filled in Zhytomyr, Zaporizhzhia, Almaty or a yard near Lviv, and a Dutch compliance firm cannot phone the sawmill, the mill's energy engineer or the buyer in Shymkent. That is real, and it is narrow: the Dutch founder still owns the importer, so every idea's step two is the same Dutch partner. Half the facts here are from memory; most fetches hit dead pages. I would put my own money into K1, the EUDR wood file: the date is verified, the goods are physical and numerous, and the first paid test costs less than a good laptop.
