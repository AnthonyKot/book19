Triage of the five research runs — Fable 5.1, from knowledge and judgment (2026-09-03)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/notes/2026-09-03-fable-triage.md.

Pool: 24 candidates across succession (codex), diaspora (codex), licences (codex), trade flows (Gemini), regulation-created work (Gemini), plus the crowdfunding operator from the earlier runs. Read in full. The runs' figures trace to saved sources; what follows is my judgment on whether the business stands up.

Keep, ranked

  1. Labour-migrant housing and agency compliance operator (regulation R1 + diaspora D4). SNF pre-audits before the 1 Oct 2026 tightening, plus arrival/onboarding execution for agencies and housing operators. Real, dated, physical, language-gated in the reader's favour (the tenants speak Polish/Ukrainian/Russian), no Dutch exam, ~20k locations, 700 registered enterprises, many Polish-owned agencies as buyers. Adds a second deadline the runs underweighted: the Wtta admission regime for temp agencies (expected 2027) puts every agency through a compliance gate. Concern: it is consulting until a team runs it; recurring via annual audits and rule changes. First test as proposed: €1,500 mock audit for three locations.
  2. Acquire an owner-independent B2B physical business (succession S1/S2): specialist distributor or B2B textile care with the Dutch account owner retained, financed with equity + Qredits/BMKB. The succession run's real finding is the rejection rule (owner-independent cash flow, retained Dutch customer ownership), and that is the chapter. Edge: capital, process discipline, CEE sourcing. Test: broker one paid order first.
  3. F-gas / heat-pump service company built by hiring or acquisition (licences L1 + regulation R2). Shortage is real (121,000), the English STEK exam is real, but the personal route is the weak part: the A1 course expects refrigeration experience and brazing; at 40 with no trade background this is a company thesis, not a career change. Policy risk the runs missed: the Dutch cabinet dropped the 2026 hybrid-heat-pump obligation in 2024; demand now rides on ISDE subsidies, which is exactly R2's kill fact.
  4. Polish joinery / structural-steel agency with the compliance dossier as the product (trade T1 + T4). Capital-light, genuine edge (Polish supplier access + Wkb/CPR/EN 1090/CBAM literacy), but crowded on the joinery side (many Dutch dealers of Polish windows exist) and thin commissions on steel. Test one brokered order with a full dossier; if the dossier is what the buyer pays for, it is a business.
  5. Acquire a certified asbestos-survey microbusiness (licences L6). Regulated, physical, shortage, AI-proof; the DIA must stay through change of control, which is the whole deal.
  6. Funeral and repatriation case desk (diaspora D2). A real, unserved, high-trust niche; small volume; partner-dependent. A chapter, not the engine.
  7. CRA importer-of-record / crowdfunding operator (trade T3 + crowdfunding). Solid, but it is technical files and SBOMs — book 18's world. Park it for a book 18 sequel; it fails this book's "outside IT" premise.

Cut, and why

  • Textile-waste aggregation to Polish sorters (R3): an existing, distressed commodity trade — post-consumer textile prices collapsed in 2023–24, Dutch and Belgian sorters went under, collectors already export east. A newcomer with €100k is the last in line, and the Waste Shipment Regulation is tightening. Not arbitrage.
  • Wkb freelance field inspector (R4): a personal role on Dutch-speaking sites for Dutch-reading clients.
  • Multilingual medical/dental intake (D1): the runs passed it on the AI gate; I fail it. Multilingual intake and callback is among the first things AI voice agents replace.
  • Mobile vehicle service (D3): the reader is not a mechanic; hire-a-mechanic at €50 call-outs is a job.
  • LED luminaire and acoustic-pod distribution (T2, T5): incumbent dealer networks; Gemini's own kill facts.
  • Drone inspection (L3), NEN 3140 service (L4), crane operator (L5): commoditised, weak, or a trade.
  • EP-W label boutique (L2): huge volume at commodity prices, Dutch-only exam, automation pressure.
  • Event rental, small-batch manufacturing, technical workshop (S3–S5): seasonal, capex-heavy or technician-dependent; fail the owner-independence rule the same run wrote.

What the five runs agree on, which is the book's thesis

No instrument found a secret. Every one found the same shape: the reader's edge is not access to a market but the combination of languages, regulatory literacy and capital applied to physical work that Dutch operators find bureaucratic and foreign makers find foreign. The essays should be the seven kept above, with the "Why you" section carrying that argument and the honesty that a Dutch founder could do most of it too.

What this needs from the author before the pitch step

The four constraints (passive vs hands-on; alongside the job or replacing it; capital ceiling; retraining). Ranks 1 and 4 are hands-on and part-time-startable; 2, 3 and 5 are capital deployments that need attention but not a trade; 6 is a side desk. The answers reorder this list.
