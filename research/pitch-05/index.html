Pitch 5 — The heat-pump service company, bought or hired (Family B)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/pitches/05-heat-pump-company.md.

The unit and the file. A small installation-and-maintenance firm for heat pumps and cooling, two to ten staff, either bought from a retiring owner or built around one certified installer; sells installations and maintenance contracts per system. The file is the F-gas certification: the company certificate plus the per-person STEK/F-gas exam, which exists in English (licences memo, verified).

The rule and the date. No hard date; the demand rides on subsidies (ISDE) and on the sector's shortage, verified at 121,000 workers. Policy risk noted in triage one: the 2026 hybrid-heat-pump obligation was dropped in 2024; net metering ends 1 January 2027 (verified, certified-object run), which shifts demand toward self-consumption and storage.

Who buys. Homeowners and small commercial owners through installers; for the reader, the sellers: 41,000 installation firms, 100 ownership changes in 2023 rising to 150–200 expected, 30 live Brookz profiles, multiples 4.3–5.5 and rising, 78% of Techniek Nederland respondents considering sale (all verified, boring-business memo).

Why you. Capital and process discipline for a trade that runs on WhatsApp; CEE technicians (the F-gas exam in English lowers the gate for them too); the reader's own literacy in the subsidy and certification rules. The Dutch gate is the household customer, which the retained owner or a Dutch-speaking foreman covers.

The precedent. The sector's consolidation itself; no single firm fetched. Triage one's warning stands: the personal route (the reader as installer) is not a career change at forty.

The ninety-day test. One subcontracted maintenance day under a certified partner, to see the work and the customer. Under €200.

Steps two and three. Step two: buy a firm with the certificate-holder retained, or hire one certified installer and run the back office. Step three: a second firm on the same back office, or cooling and data-centre maintenance contracts (AI-footprint memo lists refrigeration installers as a shortage occupation).

The AI line. Physical installation and service; AI touches scheduling and quoting, which the reader already automates.

The kill fact. The subsidy that carries demand is cut, or exits in the sector keep rising (2–10-person exit rate 2.3% → 3.4%, bankruptcies 98 → 133 in two years, verified). Both are public numbers to watch, not questions to ask.

The number to settle first. Normalised margin of a target firm after the owner's wage: sector margins are 12.6–12.9% (verified), but that is before the owner is replaced.

Verdict under the four assumptions. Ranked fifth. Strengthened as a buy, weakened as a build. Not beside a job once bought; capital is the whole ceiling plus debt; no retraining for the reader, but a certificate-holder must be kept. If the reader has a higher capital ceiling this moves up; if the ceiling is €50k it fails.
