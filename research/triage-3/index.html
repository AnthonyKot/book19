Triage of the ideation pass — Fable 5.1, from knowledge and judgment (2026-09-04, evening)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/notes/2026-09-04-fable-triage-3.md.

Pool: two Fable 5.1 ideation agents run on scripts/prompts/idea-metaprompt.md with two abstract seeds ("assembled machines": the moment a bought thing becomes something someone must sign for; "crossing flows": the moment a border turns a shipment or a person into paperwork), eight kept ideas between them; then two review agents applying the precedent rule (a named small firm elsewhere that already lives on the shape) and verifying what the ideation runs had marked unverified. The test of the method: could an agent reach the cobot CE service the author's editor had proposed, without being told? It did, in the first seed, priced against a verified Dutch incumbent (€6,000 per simple machine, €30,000 per assembly), and the review found a one-engineer firm in Corby that has lived on exactly this since 2019. The shape holds. The second seed went somewhere the editor had not: border files.

Added to the kept list

  • NEW #2. The cell file: CE for robot cells, lines and fleets assembled on SME floors. From 20 January 2027 (verified) the Machinery Regulation makes whoever assembles the cell the manufacturer of a new machine; the new EN ISO 10218-2:2025 is published. Per cell: risk assessment, validation measurements, technical file, declaration, at €1,500–3,000 against the incumbent's €6,000. Precedents: Cobots and Machinery Safety Ltd (Corby, one engineer, 2019) and Machine Safety Specialists (Ohio, 1977). Second product line from the same run: the substantial-modification file for retrofitted machines in Polish- and Ukrainian-owned workshops (new Product Liability Directive, 9 December 2026, verified; Polish "wymagania minimalne" firms as precedent). Step three, confirmed as step three and not entry: one-application palletising cells, installed and signed (Olympus Technologies, Huddersfield, £65–115k per cell). Ranked second because it is the best fit of edge to unit in the book: regulatory literacy and engineering habit, English-speaking technical buyers, no Dutch exam, tools under €10k.
  • NEW #7. Border files for CEE imports: the carbon file and the wood file. Two ideas, one desk. Dutch SME importers of Ukrainian, Turkish and Kazakh steel, aluminium and fertiliser need CBAM declarant status and verified installation emissions (50-tonne threshold verified at the Dutch emissions authority; the registration date has already passed), and the data sits in a mill that answers in Russian or Ukrainian. Importers of Ukrainian pallets, pellets and timber need the deforestation due-diligence statement with plot geolocation from 30 December 2026 (verified), and the polygons sit in Ukrainian forestry offices. Precedents: Ingdilligenz GmbH (Würzburg, 2021, sells the CBAM-representative role) and RECO (Gliwice, EUDR desk for SME importers). Honest flag: this is desk work attached to a truckload, not a site visit. It passes the book line (no software product) but sits at the edge of "physical". Kept because the language edge is the strongest in the pool and the deadlines are real.
  • NEW #8. Used-equipment inspection and the not-waste file for CEE buyers. Dutch machinery, workshop kit and dismantled greenhouse equipment sold to Poland and Ukraine needs, from 21 May 2026 (verified), a per-lot functionality record to travel as goods rather than waste, and the buyer in Poland or Ukraine wants an inspection report in their language before paying. Mevas (Germany, 2006, 700+ inspections a year, from €720 a machine) proves the per-unit economics with the payer on the buyer's side. The used-greenhouse-equipment line the author raised sits here. Physical, per unit, language edge, sanctions line drawn at Russia and Belarus.

Watch list (not ranked, one fact from entry)

  • Sanctions and dual-use order file for Dutch machinery exporters to Central Asia and the Caucasus: the rules are verified, the demand is real, but no small firm anywhere was found selling per-order end-user verification; only general export-control shops and law firms. Leaves watch when one is named.
  • EU import, CE and service hub for machinery-class crowdfunding products: the best precedent of the pass (iGo3D, Hannover, 2013, 40–50 staff, own repair workshop), but the kill fact is half true: the large raisers already run EU stores. Only the mid-tier is open. A research run should list the machinery-class campaigns over $3m from 2024–2026 without an EU entity.
  • Battery-pack CE and passport file for packs assembled by Dutch e-boat yards and forklift electrifiers: no small firm lives on it, and the passport half is software-adjacent.

Cut

  • Notified-body file for machine-learning safety functions: no precedent, the date corrected to 2 August 2028, and the work is data validation. Book 18's world.

What the pass says about the method

The metaprompt reproduces the editor's best idea from an abstract seed, which means the shape (iron plus paper, dated rule, small English-speaking buyers, no Dutch exam, per-unit price, AI adds units, one kill fact, one precedent) is the book's actual selection rule and can be stated as such in the method chapter. The precedent rule did the most work: it sorted eight ideas into three tiers in one pass and cut the one that sounded best on paper. Cost: four agents, roughly 400k tokens.
