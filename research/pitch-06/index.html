Pitch 6 — The joinery and steel dossier (Family A)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/pitches/06-joinery-steel-dossier.md.

The unit and the file. One order of Polish windows, doors, façade elements or structural-steel subassemblies for a Dutch builder, delivered with the complete file: CE and declaration of performance under the Construction Products Regulation, EN 1090 execution class for steel, the Wkb quality-assurance evidence the builder must hand to the municipality, and the CBAM data for steel. Sold as a brokerage commission plus a fixed fee for the dossier (estimate: 5–8% and €1,500–4,000 per order).

The rule and the date. The Dutch building-quality act (Wkb) in force since 2024 for the lowest consequence class, with the quality assurer needing a file per project (verified, regulation memo); CBAM definitive regime from 1 January 2026 (verified); EN 1090 as the condition for CE on structural steel (verified).

Who buys. Dutch builders and developers of ten to a hundred staff who buy Polish joinery and steel for the price and cannot produce the file themselves; the trade-flow memo shows Dutch imports of Polish aluminium joinery and steel subassemblies rising 2023–2025 (verified). The incumbents are the established Dutch dealers of Polish windows, which is the crowding risk.

Why you. Polish supplier access in Polish; regulatory literacy across four rule sets in one file; engineering habit for the evidence trail. No Dutch exam. The Dutch gate is the builder's project manager, who works in Dutch and needs a Dutch-speaking partner from step two.

The precedent. No named small firm fetched; the incumbents are the precedent and the problem. A research run should find one dealer whose product is the dossier rather than the window.

The ninety-day test. Broker one order with a full dossier for a fee, through a builder in the reader's network. Under €1,000.

Steps two and three. Step two: a Dutch-speaking partner on the builder side; the dossier as a standalone service for orders the reader did not broker. Step three: the importer of record for two or three Polish fabricators, holding stock or not.

The AI line. Physical goods with a file per order; AI shortens the file's assembly, which is the reader's margin, and the rules keep adding fields (CBAM, the digital product passport).

The kill fact. Buyers pay for the goods, not the dossier; the dealers already include a file good enough for the quality assurer. Ask two quality assurers what they reject and why.

The number to settle first. The share of Polish joinery and steel orders in the Netherlands that arrive without a compliant CPR/EN 1090 file, as seen by quality assurers.

Verdict under the four assumptions. Ranked sixth: capital-light, beside a job, no retraining, hands-on. It rises if the dossier proves to be what buyers pay for; it falls to watch if the incumbents already ship a good-enough file.
