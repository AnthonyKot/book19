Crowdfunding research — codex vs Gemini, compared (2026-09-03)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/notes/2026-09-03-comparison.md.

Same brief, same datasets (Web Robots Kickstarter 2026-08-12 and 2023-12-14, Indiegogo 2025-10-13, public Asian rankings). Both runs' headline figures trace to the saved files (spot-checked by the coordinating session). Outputs: drafts/reviews/crowdfunding-findings.md (codex, with a traceability test script) and drafts/reviews/crowdfunding-findings-agy.md (Gemini 3.8 Flash).

Where they agree

  • 88% of physical-product Kickstarter money since 2023 is non-EU, but the top tier is mature Shenzhen/US brands that already have EU entities and Amazon listings within months.
  • Of the top 40 proven products, only a handful lack an EU seller (codex: 35 established, 2 direct-only, 3 no retail; Gemini's table shows a similar minority of NO/PARTIAL rows).
  • BB-777 boombox (US, $7.13m, 9,334 backers, no EU distributor) appears on both lists.
  • The viable shape is EU distribution + compliance for a foreign maker stalled by EU rules, not wholesale arbitrage; connected devices are where the CRA (Sept 2026) makes an EU importer both valuable and liable.

Where they differ

  • Verdict emphasis. Codex: "mostly things Europe already sells; outreach leads, not validated businesses." Gemini: "3–5 ideas, a sniper business." Same facts, different tone; codex's is the safer reading for a book.
  • Candidates. Codex: NestWorks C500 CNC, COFO Chair Pro 2 (JP), il modo Air wallets (JP), BB-777, Night Storm X3. Gemini: JetKVM, Willcook microwave bag (JP), iMini F1 intercom (TW), BB-777. Codex leans to machinery/furniture/soft goods with lower compliance risk (GPSR, REACH, packaging); Gemini leans to connected electronics (CRA/RED) where the moat is larger and so is the liability.
  • JetKVM is on Gemini's list, not codex's: it sells D2C worldwide and via AliExpress resellers, so "no EU distributor" is true but "no EU availability" is not.

What this says for book 19

The instrument works: paid demand, dated, per product, with an EU-seller check. The business it points at is narrow: an EU-distribution-and-compliance operator for foreign makers, tested one maker at a time. Next step if pursued: pick two candidates (one connected, one not), write the maker outreach and the compliance-cost estimate as a book-18-style paid diagnostic, and run the A2 and AI gates on it as an essay.
