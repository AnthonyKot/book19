Pitch 10 — The spray crew's file (Family A)

A research memo, not an essay. Written by an agent from public sources on the date stated; every figure is meant to trace to a saved excerpt in the sources/ folder beside this page. Source file in the repository: drafts/pitches/10-spray-crew-file.md.

The unit and the file. For one crew that sprays polyurethane foam, applies PU coatings or paints vehicles: the diisocyanate exposure assessment, the exposure register, the training records the REACH restriction requires, and the annual review; sold per crew per year (estimate €1,500–3,000, with measurement days extra). Second line, same kit and same customers: the lead and chromium file for blasting, demolition and strip-out crews.

The rule and the date. Directive (EU) 2024/869, transposed in the Netherlands and in force 9 April 2026, transitional values to 31 December 2028 (verified twice, Arboportaal and the legislative calendar); the REACH training duty for diisocyanate users since 24 August 2023, with a five-year certificate (verified).

Who buys. Insulation contractors, coating applicators and body shops with five to fifty staff; many of the crews are Polish- and Ukrainian-speaking, and some of the firms are Polish-owned. Too small for the large occupational-health providers; exposed to the Labour Inspectorate after 2026.

Why you. The crews are the reader's languages, and the training and the register are half conversation. Regulatory literacy reads the directive and the Dutch transposition directly; engineering habit runs the sampling protocol and the register. The Dutch gate is real and named: the Inspectorate expects the assessment at occupational-hygienist level, so a registered hygienist signs from the first sale. The reader sells and runs the file; the hygienist signs it.

The precedent. Synergy Environmental Solutions Ltd, Stoke-on-Trent, incorporated 2012 (Companies House, re-traced through two directories); EHS Analytical Solutions, San Diego, air sampling and blood-lead rounds (traced through federal contracting records); Respiratory Protective Assessment Ltd, Bristol, for the mask line.

The ninety-day test. One paid exposure survey and training-record audit for one spray-foam contractor, with a hygienist on a day rate. Under €2,000 including the hygienist.

Steps two and three. Step two: a retained hygienist and a book of twenty crews across foam, coating and body shops. Step three: the iron: local-exhaust-ventilation testing per system and respirator fit-testing per face, both on watch until a Dutch rule names them, and both sold to the same crews.

The AI line. A sampling pump on a person on a site; AI writes the register and reads the transitional values. More rules, more fields, same site visit.

The kill fact. The Inspectorate accepts modelled exposure without measurement, which the review found half true: models are accepted, but multi-day measurement is expected near the limits. If the large occupational-health providers bundle the file into their existing contracts at no charge, the niche closes. Two contractor calls and one provider's price list.

The number to settle first. How many Dutch firms use diisocyanates in spray form, and how many have the REACH training certificates on file. The training providers know the second.

Verdict under the four assumptions. Ranked tenth and last of the pitches: beside a job, capital under €5k plus a rented pump, no retraining, hands-on. It sits last because the Dutch gate is a partner rather than a paper. It rises if the reader takes the hygienist qualification, which is exactly the two-year retraining the assumptions rule out.
