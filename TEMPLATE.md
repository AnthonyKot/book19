# Book 19 — essay template (book 18's spine plus one section)

Kicker: `Essay N of 6 · <candidate name> · from pitch P`

H1: an argued title, not the candidate label.

Lede (`p.lede`, ≤130 words): the purchase decision and the tension that could make the idea
attractive or fatal for this reader.

1. **The proposition** (`section.proposition`) — one buyer, purchase event, deliverable,
   substitute, initial boundary. What the business refuses to be (never software with a login).
2. **What changed** (`section.changed`) — the dated rule or the survival data, with the
   obligation, the enforcement and the budget separated.
3. **Why you** (`section.whyyou`) — the edge that does the work here, from the edge inventory;
   the Dutch-language gate for this exact channel, answered honestly (a paper, a partner, or a
   fail); the precedent firm elsewhere with its trace; and the constraint that would break it.
4. **The first sale** (`section.sale`) — ten named-prospect types, the fixed paid diagnostic,
   who signs and why now, language and geography.
5. **Does the money work?** (`section.economics`) — bottom-up year 1 and a plausible mature
   year, sourced prices separated from required-price arithmetic, working capital, the
   ≤EUR 50k-at-risk rule applied.
6. **From service to company** (`section.scale`) — first, fifth and twentieth customer; what
   stays human, what is delegated, what asset accumulates, the founder bottleneck.
7. **The incumbent gets a turn** (`section.competition`) — named competitors and substitutes,
   their response, partner capture.
8. **The capable failure** (`div.counter`) — a non-straw-man path where good execution loses.
9. **The first ninety days** (`section.monday`) — 4–6 numbered actions, maximum pre-evidence
   spend (≤EUR 5,000), paid test, pass rule, kill rule; alongside the day job.
10. **Verdict** (`div.verdict-box`) — one exact verdict string from CONTEXT.md and the single
    assumption that would most change it.
11. **Reading** (`div.reading`) — 5–10 primary sources actually used.
