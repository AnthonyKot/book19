The Third Engine

Book 4 asked how firms survive. Book 18 asked which technical second business deserves two years and a bounded stake. This book asks the question those two left open: what can a forty-year-old enterprise developer in the Netherlands, with A2 Dutch, three other languages and a hundred thousand euros, earn that does not run on code and still exists in 2030? This is version 0.1. There are no essays yet. There is the method, the reader's edge inventory, ten research memos written by agents from public sources, and a triage of what they found. The essays come after the author's pick.

The ruleDemand, payment, economics, operations and resilience, in that order, as in Book 18. Two gates on top: the A2 gate, can it run with A2 Dutch or does a partner own the Dutch customer; and the AI gate, does AI progress raise or lower demand for it by 2030. No instrument found a secret. Every one found the same shape: languages plus regulatory literacy plus capital, applied to physical work that Dutch operators find bureaucratic and foreign makers find foreign.

What v0.1 is not

It is not advice and not a verdict. The memos are agent research with saved sources and stated limits; the triage is one editor's judgment; the essays that test each candidate against demand, payment, economics, operations and resilience do not exist yet. When they do, they replace this page's table one row at a time.

Nineteenth in a series built the same way. Corrections welcome.
