Essay 6 of 6 · Certified inspection microfirm · from pitch 9

The certificate is not the asset. The second qualified employee is.

Before an older Dutch building is renovated or demolished, somebody may need to inspect it for asbestos and put a qualified name on the report. The apparent business is durable: buy a small certified bureau, keep the inspector, and own the customer book. The dangerous word is keep. A2 Dutch cannot replace the inspector, the technical signatory or the customer lead. One departure can stop production. This essay narrows the pitch to asbestos inventory and asks whether a two-inspector firm can pay both people, acquisition debt and an owner who has left a developer's salary.

The proposition

The target is a Dutch asbestos-inventory bureau, not an asbestos-removal contractor and not a general inspection holding company. It has two employed DIA certificate-holders, a Dutch planner or account owner, a valid company process certificate and several repeat B2B accounts. The buyer takes the shares, the team, the quality system, the work history and the trading name.

The end customer is usually a property, maintenance or project manager. The purchase event is a planned renovation, maintenance opening or demolition involving a pre-1994 building. The immediate deliverable is a scoped site investigation, laboratory-backed source record and signed asbestos-inventory report. The regulated company must employ at least one DIA; the scheme also requires a DIA to perform the inventory.

The technical final responsible person is more than a decorative certificate-holder. That person must be a DIA, must meet an experience or annual-work test, must know the quality system and signs the final report. The business therefore sells field judgement plus a controlled file. Scheduling, pre-research and report assembly can improve. Sampling, exceptions and the signature remain human.

The first substitute is another certified local bureau. The second is a larger environmental consultancy that can add soil, chromium-6, occupational hygiene or project supervision. The third is the customer's preferred demolition contractor arranging a separate inventory firm. The new owner does not sell removal, laboratory analysis, legal advice or a certificate in their own name. It never becomes software with a login.

This boundary also excludes the seductive one-person target. A profitable owner-DIA practice is a job wearing a company number. If the seller is the only person who can visit, judge and sign, the customer book and process certificate do not become owner-independent merely because the share transfer completes.

What changed

The obligation is standing, not new. Article 7.9 of the Besluit bouwwerken leefomgeving requires the party doing or commissioning demolition to hold an asbestos-inventory report for the affected part when asbestos is known or suspected. Buildings from before 1994 create that presumption. Post-1 January 1994 buildings and specified exceptions fall outside the general rule. The report must reach the contractor before removal.

This separates obligation from purchase event. Owning an old building creates no automatic annual inspection budget. A renovation, maintenance opening or demolition project activates the queue. The project owner or coordinator commissions the inventory; the inventory findings and report then enter the national asbestos tracking system. Enforcement can make the file necessary, but the project's timing and procurement route decide who gets paid.

The latest process-scheme amendment was published on 26 August 2026 and took effect the next day. It updated the reference to the 2025 version of NEN 2990. The official explanation says the change was limited and added no administrative burden. There is no fresh regulatory wave to sell. The business must win on response time, trusted reports and repeat property work.

The existing control is substantial. The certificate-holder must report changes of directors, management, owners and ultimate beneficiaries immediately to its certification body. A process certificate lasts at most three years and the certification body reviews every branch at least annually, including a sample of files. A share purchase can preserve the legal entity, but it does not make change of control invisible.

Ascert's live register showed 155 certified asbestos-inventory firms and 798 valid DIA certificates when checked. Personal DIA certificates generally last three years. Those are supply counts, not evidence of a shortage or of sellers. They also average several DIA certificates per firm only arithmetically; the distribution and employment status are not public.

Buyer behaviour exists but is narrow. HMP says it structurally works with self-employed DIAs and offers regular assignments around Eindhoven, Arnhem and Nijmegen inside its certified organisation. This proves that a certified bureau can buy variable inspection capacity. It does not prove that a stranger can acquire the bureau, retain its employed DIA or win the same work.

The pitch's kill fact is therefore only partly tested. The scheme confirms that losing the employed DIA is operationally fatal until another qualifying employee is in place. The public record cannot show whether a particular target's DIA will stay, on what package, or whether customers will follow a non-DIA owner. Those facts require a named target, employment documents and direct conversations.

Why you

Capital and process discipline do the work here. The reader can map every report from quote, building history and site scope through sample identity, laboratory result, risk class, exception, signature and archive. The scheme requires a quality system, controlled documents, internal review, training records and long report retention. That is familiar systems work applied to a physical service, not an excuse to build an app.

Polish and Russian can help with CEE-owned contractors, farms, logistics buildings and property operators. English helps with institutional owners. Those languages do not replace the Dutch customer layer. Residents, municipal staff, contractors and certification auditors can all need precise Dutch. A misunderstood access limit or excluded room is not a cosmetic translation error.

The personal Dutch gate is a fail. ProXia requires a DIA candidate to command Dutch in speech and writing; the examination includes theory, report and practical components. At A2, the reader does not inspect or sign. The business gate passes only through retained people: an employed DIA, a qualifying DIA as technical final responsible person, and a Dutch account owner. Put all three names and deputies in the investment memo. If one person fills all three roles, fail the target.

The precedent is Advies Lieren's acquisition of Buro Pear on 17 August 2026. The announcement says Buro Pear's seller initially kept daily leadership of ongoing projects, staff work remained unchanged, and the work would later transfer to Advies Lieren's principals. Ascert independently listed both Advies Lieren and Buro Pear with valid inventory certificates after the announced date.

That precedent is unusually useful because it argues against a lazy version of the thesis. Advies Lieren was already a certified operator with its own capacity. It could absorb work and transfer leadership gradually. The record proves that the deal shape exists. It does not prove that an A2 financial buyer without a qualified bench can copy it.

The constraint that breaks the fit is the day job. The ninety-day search can run beside employment; ownership cannot. Customer calls, staff safety, audit findings and site exceptions do not wait for evenings. This is step two: resign only at a signed, financed closing, and only after the qualified employees sign retention agreements.

The first sale

Build ten prospect types around physical projects: housing-corporation maintenance teams, VvE managers, commercial-property managers, demolition estimators, renovation contractors, roofers, installers opening old risers or plant rooms, municipal estate teams, farm-building owners and CEE-owned logistics or light-industrial landlords. Start in North Holland, where site visits remain compatible with a day job.

The pre-acquisition product is one brokered inspection, not an uncertified report. A certified partner contracts with the customer, scopes the work, visits, samples and signs. The reader qualifies the project and receives a fixed €100 completed-introduction hurdle from the partner. The test job should involve a pre-1994 small commercial unit with a real works date, not a hypothetical asbestos question.

A public 2026 price list from Asdesk gives simple-building comparators of €450 including VAT for a field shed, stable, detached garage or object, and €515 including VAT for a house, each with one sample. Extra locations and samples cost more. Those are observed list prices, not this essay's B2B average. The partner sets and invoices the real test price. The €100 introduction fee is a hurdle.

The person who signs the purchase is the property or project manager controlling the works schedule. The reason to act is not fear of asbestos in general. It is that the contractor cannot proceed responsibly through the affected area without the required scope and report. The first call asks for building year, work boundary, access, drawings, intended start, known materials and the person who can approve destructive investigation.

After acquisition, the bureau sells the inspection directly. The founder should not become the Dutch call centre. The retained planner owns intake, scope confirmation and complaints; the DIA owns technical qualification and report. Polish or English is an additional acquisition route, never the safety language by default.

At least one certified firm must agree to the referral arrangement in writing before outreach. The customer must see who is certified and who carries responsibility. If every partner rejects paid introductions, do not disguise lead generation as a diagnostic. The experiment then failed to establish a lawful, paid wedge.

Does the money work?

The first number is now a screen. A current employer advertises €2,500–€4,375 gross per month for a 32–40 hour DIA role. Use a €75,000 loaded annual retention-cost hurdle for a senior signing DIA after salary, employer costs, pension, vehicle and equipment. Only the salary range is observed. The loaded amount is deliberately conservative required-price arithmetic.

If the seller is the only technical final responsible person and their labour is missing from payroll, reported EBITDA must be at least €150,000 before that normalisation. Subtract the €75,000 role cost; otherwise the signature consumes more than half the profit. If a senior DIA's full cost is already in payroll, count only the incremental retention package. This settles the pitch's number as a due-diligence rule, not as a claim about any unnamed target.

The first year is thin even after paying the founder €85,000 at replacement cost. Five hundred jobs mean about five completed jobs per DIA per week across fifty working weeks. The model dies if the verified job mix cannot average €900 before VAT, if reporting time lowers capacity, or if rework and samples exceed the allowances.

The acquisition-price hurdle is €300,000: no more than €50,000 reader equity at risk, a €200,000 loan and a €50,000 seller earn-out paid only when retained staff and customer gross profit persist. Qredits currently publishes €50,000–€250,000 MKB credit, 9.95% annual interest, 1.5% one-off cost, an average five-year term and acquisition as an allowed use. A €200,000 five-year annuity at that published rate is about €50,900 annual debt service; that calculation is not an approval or quote.

Brookz's broad construction-and-installation guidance gives an indicative 4.3–5.5 EBITDA multiple. Its visible asbest page showed two much larger active businesses and one sold inventory bureau at €850,000 revenue and 6–10 staff. The record did not show an affordable two-DIA firm, a completed price or verified profit. At the low multiple, €70,000 normalised operating surplus supports roughly the €300,000 hurdle. If the seller applies the multiple before paying replacement labour, walk away.

Working capital needs its own reserve. Bill scheduled portfolio work monthly, collect deposits on private jobs and keep customer-funded laboratory work short-cycle. The €50,000 equity cannot all become goodwill. Legal, financial, tax, employment and certificate diligence, the lender fee and at least one month of payroll must fit inside the purchase structure. No deal closes if doing so pushes reader capital at risk above €50,000.

From service to company

Customer one teaches intake. Record who owns the building, what will be disturbed, which drawings exist, what is excluded, who grants access and who accepts the report. The founder observes but does not overrule the DIA. The asset is a clean, auditable hand-off from commercial scope to technical scope.

Customer five should be a second repeat channel, not five friendly jobs from one demolition firm. The Dutch planner qualifies ordinary requests. A DIA owns the plan, site work and exceptions. Laboratory orders, photographs, samples and report components move through one controlled checklist. The founder handles cash, capacity, recruitment and key-account review.

Customer twenty is a portfolio relationship with predictable building data and works planning. The accumulated asset is not the certificates; people can leave with those. It is the employed bench, renewal calendar, report corpus, route density, customer trust, calibrated equipment record and a quality system that survives audit without the founder.

Scale is constrained by the scheme. An inventory owner cannot also own a removal company. Owning two inventory companies is generally barred unless the concern meets high DIA and report-volume thresholds. This is not a casual roll-up. Expand one certified entity, or add services that preserve independence.

Audit load also steps up with delivery. The current table requires four annual project-site assessments for a branch with one or two DIAs and fewer than 500 reports. It rises to six for three to five DIAs or 500–1,100 reports. Growth buys more announced and unannounced scrutiny, not merely utilisation.

The founder bottleneck is retaining qualified judgement while refusing to interfere with it. Hiring, route design, debtor control and portfolio sales can be delegated. The technical signature cannot be automated into an owner permission. If customer twenty still calls the seller personally, the acquisition has not transferred.

The incumbent gets a turn

The strongest competitor is another certified acquirer. Advies Lieren's purchase of Buro Pear shows the response: combine specialist knowledge and customers, keep project leadership during transition, then move work onto an existing qualified bench. Such a buyer can pay for synergies that the reader does not have.

Broader firms compete differently. Tritium markets asbestos work inside a team spanning soil, environment and sustainability, and its DIA vacancy describes projects from buildings to whole housing districts. A property manager may prefer one supplier across hazardous materials and environmental work. A demolition contractor can repeatedly introduce its established independent inventory bureau without owning it.

The small incumbent can respond too. Asdesk publishes fixed prices and promises an experienced certified inspector, accredited laboratory work and a clear report. HMP advertises structural access to freelancers. One has transparent retail acquisition; the other has variable capacity. The buyer must find a target with a transferable customer book, not assume that better scheduling is novel.

Partner capture begins before purchase. An overflow partner learns which leads convert and can decline the introduction fee. After acquisition, demolition firms and property managers can demand lower prices because they control job flow. Laboratories can lengthen turnaround or raise sample charges. None of these parties needs to buy the company for its margin to shrink.

The capable failure

The buyer finds a respected two-DIA bureau. Both inspectors sign retention agreements. Customers accept the change, reports remain sound and audits pass. The reader improves scheduling and wins several portfolio accounts. Execution is competent.

A certified strategic buyer could still have paid more. It already has technical cover, Dutch management, a quality function and spare route capacity. It can spread those costs across more reports and move work when one DIA is ill. The reader must fund the same resilience inside a small standalone company.

To win the auction, the reader accepts the seller's EBITDA before replacing the seller's work and borrows against it. Twelve months later the seller leaves as agreed. A retained DIA becomes technical final responsible, receives the deserved raise and reduces field days for review. Revenue holds, yet replacement labour and debt service consume the cash. Nothing failed in sales, safety or diligence. The wrong owner had a structurally higher cost base than the strategic bidder.

The first ninety days

  1. Days 1–15: call three certification bodies, five owners from the public register and two acquisition advisers. Confirm in writing how a share transfer, director change, technical-final-responsible change and employee departure are handled. Spend at most a €500 advice-and-travel hurdle.
  2. Days 10–30: recruit one certified partner. Agree a transparent €100 completed-introduction hurdle, customer ownership, data handling and the rule that the partner scopes, contracts, inspects and signs. The reader never appears certified.
  3. Days 20–50: approach the ten prospect types. Qualify five real pre-1994 projects with planned works. Broker one inspection at the partner's quoted price and obtain the introduction fee only after the customer pays.
  4. Days 30–65: screen fifteen certified firms from the register. Seek two with at least two employed DIAs, a separate Dutch customer owner and repeat B2B revenue. Reject seller-only practices before asking price.
  5. Days 50–80: offer a €2,500 diligence-budget hurdle for an accountant and employment or certification specialist to review one anonymised target pack. Rebuild three years of EBITDA after €75,000 per missing senior-DIA role and €85,000 founder replacement.
  6. Days 75–90: ask the best owner and each critical employee separately about twelve-month plans, pay, authority and change of control. Draft a non-binding structure capped at €50,000 reader equity, with retention and customer continuity as closing conditions. Total pre-evidence spend stays below €4,500. Do not resign or sign a purchase agreement.

Pass: one real customer pays the certified partner, the partner pays the introduction fee, and one target supplies payroll, certificate, job-level margin and customer-retention evidence. It has two independently staying DIAs, one qualifying as technical final responsible, and shows at least €150,000 EBITDA before any missing seller-DIA normalisation. The €300,000 structure must still pay founder replacement and debt service. Kill: no paid inspection after five qualified projects; no target has two staying DIAs; the only technical signatory is the departing seller; normalised cash misses debt service; or all credible targets require more than €50,000 reader capital at risk. The experiment runs beside the job. Closing does not.

Verdict

ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD

The standing obligation, employed-DIA rule and physical report make this a real operating business, but they do not grant the reader a personal route. Buy only a team and customer book that already work without the seller. The single assumption that would most change the verdict is that an affordable target exists with two independently staying DIAs and at least €150,000 of pre-normalisation EBITDA. If the search finds only owner-DIA practices or strategic-buyer prices, do not enter.
