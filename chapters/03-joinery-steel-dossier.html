Essay 3 of 6 · The joinery and steel dossier · from pitch 6

The file is valuable only when it is missing

A small Dutch builder can buy windows from Poland and save money on the object, then lose the saving while matching declarations, performance values and installation evidence to a live project. The proposed business closes that gap in Polish and hands the builder a Dutch-ready evidence pack. Yet the strongest official guidance says correct manufacturer instructions may already give the quality assurer sufficient proof. Large factories publish documents and sell through distributors. The purchase decision is therefore not “does Wkb create paperwork?” It is whether a builder importing directly will pay a separate fee before one incomplete order delays approval or creates rework.

The proposition

The first buyer is the project manager at a Dutch contractor building ground-level homes, holiday units or a small industrial building. The company buys project-specific exterior windows and doors directly from a Polish factory. It has no Polish-speaking procurement employee and no internal product-compliance specialist. Its quality assurer has already named the evidence needed for the project.

The purchase event comes before the goods order. A quotation is attractive, but the product references, declared performance and installation method have not yet been reconciled with the drawings and the quality plan. The buyer can either send questions across three organisations or pay one party to close the order gate.

The fixed deliverable has two stages. A pre-order gate maps each opening to the quotation, product identity, required performance and available declaration. It records missing evidence, owners and due dates. A completion pack then matches the delivered units to the approved schedule, preserves the manufacturer’s instructions, indexes installation photographs and lists unresolved exceptions. It supports the builder and quality assurer; it is not their declaration.

The boundary matters. The service does not manufacture, install or certify a window. It does not issue a Declaration of Performance, decide structural adequacy or act as the quality assurer. The buyer contracts and pays the factory directly. Freight, defects, design changes and installation remain with the named commercial parties. This company sells installed-iron evidence, never software with a login.

Start with non-fire-rated exterior joinery in new consequence-class-1 projects. Structural steel is not an adjacent SKU. Its design authority, fabrication controls and site evidence are different. The Commission’s harmonised-standard notice lists EN 14351-1 for windows and doors and EN 1090-1 for conformity assessment of structural components. Steel becomes a later line only with a Dutch structural engineer and a fabricator already working within the required system.

The substitute is formidable: the factory’s export desk, an established Dutch dealer, the builder’s work preparer and the quality assurer’s own checklist. The dossier company wins only on awkward direct orders that cross those boundaries. If the factory and dealer already make the file usable, there is nothing left to sell.

What changed

The Wkb rules began on 1 January 2024 for new buildings in consequence class 1. That class includes ground-level homes and certain simple industrial buildings. Renovation remains postponed without a fixed start date, and higher consequence classes await evaluation. This is a narrow trigger, not a mandate covering every replacement-window job.

For a covered project, the quality assurer checks the work during construction and issues a statement when there is justified confidence that the building meets the technical rules. Before use, the client submits a completion notification. The authority receives the assurer’s statement plus specified information on risks, structure, ventilation, energy, environmental performance, fire safety and equivalent measures. An incomplete notification prevents the two-week clock to lawful use from starting.

That “dossier bevoegd gezag” is not a sack containing every certificate for every product. The private consumer or handover dossier is also distinct. It includes drawings, calculations, materials, installations, functions and use and maintenance information. The municipality and quality assurer are not parties to that private dossier, although some material can be reused for the completion notification.

Construction-product rules still create real inputs. Dutch government guidance names windows as construction products and says a Declaration of Performance is mandatory, with the manufacturer stating the product’s performance. TloKB adds the operational point: where a builder installs a product according to the manufacturer’s instructions, that can be sufficient evidence of product performance and processing. A deviation can require the builder to provide its own substantiation. The opportunity sits in matching and exceptions, not inventing certificates.

There is a substantial physical corridor. Eurostat reports 2025 Dutch imports from Poland of €160.0 million under HS 7308 iron or steel structures and parts, and €148.3 million under HS 7610 aluminium structures and parts. Both headings include far more than the proposed window orders. They prove goods move, not that Dutch builders bought them directly, that files were deficient or that a separate budget exists.

CBAM should leave the launch offer. The definitive regime began on 1 January 2026 and applies to EU importers bringing covered goods into the EU. The current single mass threshold is more than 50 tonnes, with iron, steel and aluminium among the sectors. A Polish-to-Dutch movement is intra-EU. Any CBAM duty arose at the external EU border, so a Dutch joinery dossier should not casually promise a new CBAM filing for each Polish order.

The number the pitch needed first was the share of Polish joinery and steel orders arriving without a usable CPR or EN 1090 file, as observed by quality assurers. The public record reviewed here does not publish that numerator and denominator. It also does not show how often a file problem delayed a declaration. The number remains unsettled and must be measured from case files, not replaced by import value.

Why you

Polish does useful work on the supply side. The founder can challenge a quotation, obtain a product-specific declaration, resolve conflicting identifiers and ask a factory engineer why the installation instruction differs from the drawing. Regulatory literacy keeps the request tied to the project requirement. The engineering habit turns messages and PDFs into a controlled acceptance record.

The Dutch gate is not an exam; it is the customer channel. The buyer, site supervisor and quality assurer discuss risk, deviations and responsibility in Dutch. A2 is not enough for a disputed site meeting or a sales call about liability. From the first paid order, a Dutch-speaking construction-quality consultant must lead those meetings under a fixed fee. The founder keeps the contract and the Polish factory relationship. If no credible consultant accepts that boundary, the channel fails.

FENBRO is the closest precedent found elsewhere. Its site describes selling Polish joinery abroad and a complete Irish service of product, delivery, installation and warranty. It identifies its company at Ludna 2/320 in Warsaw. Companies House independently records a same-named FENBRO LTD with two Polish directors at 2 Lok. 320, Ludna in Warsaw. An April 2026 accounts filing describes the UK company as dormant through March 2026. That is a trace, not proof of attractive economics. More importantly, FENBRO sells the whole transaction. No independently traced small firm was found whose principal product is a separately priced dossier.

The edge breaks if the reader will not work through a Dutch lead or cannot visit projects during business hours. A perfect Polish supplier call does not replace a Dutch conversation when the quality assurer flags an exception.

The first sale

Build a list around ten prospect types: modular-home builders, timber-frame contractors, holiday-home builders, small developer-contractors, self-build general contractors, agricultural-building contractors, two-storey warehouse builders, light-workshop builders, facade subcontractors and import-direct window installers serving new homes. Exclude consumer replacement work, public tenders and projects outside consequence class 1.

The signer is the project manager or operations director who controls the purchase order and can require evidence before release. Approach through Dutch construction-quality consultants, quality-assurer introductions and the founder’s professional network. The message names one order and one acceptance risk. It does not promise cheaper Polish windows or “Wkb compliance.” Polish factories are suppliers, not leads to be resold.

The first offer is a pre-order evidence gate at a €2,000 required-price hurdle, 50% in advance. It covers one quotation, one building and up to twenty openings. The completion pack is a separate €3,000 hurdle. Both prices are test design, not observed market rates. The buyer receives the index, source documents and exception log even if it stops.

That separation tests the kill fact. A commission on goods can hide whether the file has value. A paid evidence line cannot. The factory and builder contract directly; the service never finances the goods. If the buyer accepts only a percentage hidden inside the window price, the thesis has become ordinary brokerage.

Year one is a paid experiment beside the job, not owner income. It loses €23,600 after valuing founder time. Cash loss is lower because that labour is not a payroll cheque, but ignoring it would disguise a weak practice. The model has passed only when customers pay both stages without a goods margin subsidising them.

The mature hurdle assumes ten completed orders a month. At twenty-four delivery hours per order, delivery consumes 240 hours monthly. Two coordinators and one quality lead each provide a designed 120 productive hours, leaving one-third of capacity for exceptions, training and absences. The founder sells, handles escalations and maintains supplier relationships. If average delivery exceeds thirty hours, gross margin falls below the intended level before overhead.

The €100,000 founder line is replacement-cost compensation before personal tax. The €120,000 operating surplus is separate and must fund tax, hiring variance and retained capital. There is no recurring revenue in the base case. Repeat projects and supplier familiarity may lower acquisition and delivery cost, but each fee must be earned on an order.

Working capital is controlled by refusing title to the goods, billing each service stage 50% in advance and using contractors until receipts support employment. Before hiring, use a €25,000 reserve hurdle for late customer payments, rework and professional fees. Year-one cash at risk stays below €50,000 only if no factory deposit, freight bill or warranty claim enters the company. Crossing that boundary changes the business into a distributor and requires a new liability and capital case.

From service to company

Customer one is founder-led. The durable output is a field map: drawing reference, opening, product code, required performance, declared performance, instruction revision, delivery identity, installation evidence, exception, owner and decision. The Dutch reviewer signs only their own professional judgement. The factory remains responsible for its documents.

Customer five uses a written intake, naming convention and exception taxonomy. A bilingual coordinator chases routine evidence. The founder handles supplier engineering questions; the Dutch lead handles buyer and assurer meetings. At least half of delivery hours must now occur without the founder. Otherwise Polish fluency is a job, not a company asset.

Customer twenty should reveal repetition across several factories. The accumulating asset is not an app. It is a verified supplier-document map, typical response times, recurring identifier errors, accepted installation evidence patterns and trained coordinators. Customer project data remains separated and exportable. Ordinary document storage can support the operation; customers never need another login.

Scale is earned if one coordinator can run four live orders, supplier familiarity reduces missing-document time, and the Dutch lead reviews exceptions rather than every line. It fails if each quality assurer demands a unique format, every project needs senior engineering judgement or factories refuse reuse. In those cases, the twentieth delivery still resembles the first.

The incumbent gets a turn

Eko-Okna already presents a Dutch-language export channel, direct contact for new business customers, a distributor platform and a document library containing product-parameter certificates. This is the kill fact in public view. The factory can place documents next to the order without a specialist intermediary.

FENBRO bundles quotation, delivery, installation and warranty around Polish joinery. A Dutch wholesaler such as Kozijnen Unie markets Dutch-speaking staff, supply, project guidance and its own installation team. Those businesses can include document chasing in product margin. The proposed company asks the buyer to see and pay for the line separately.

The internal substitute is one good work preparer using the factory portal and the quality assurer’s checklist. The assurer may identify gaps during ordinary project review. A facade subcontractor may already own measurement and installation evidence. For structural steel, the certified fabricator and structural engineer sit closer to the design decision than a broker does.

The Dutch-speaking partner can also capture the margin. After learning which Polish contacts respond and which documents matter, that consultant can hire a Polish coordinator or offer the pack free with broader Wkb work. Protecting introductions contractually will not fix weak buyer value. The defence is faster closure across repeated suppliers, transparent fixed fees and direct ownership of the builder contract. The ninety-day test must show that this is enough.

The capable failure

The company executes cleanly. It recruits a respected Dutch quality lead, answers in both languages, obtains every declaration and leaves immaculate indexes. Builders like the service. Quality assurers accept the packs. Yet almost every factory file was already substantively adequate. The real work was naming files and forwarding installation photographs.

Dealers then copy the checklist and include it with the windows. Factories improve their Dutch portals because missing documents slow their own collections. Quality assurers standardise evidence requests. Only unusual products and site deviations still need help, and those cases consume senior judgement. The average fee falls while liability anxiety rises.

Nothing failed in execution. The service trained the channel to absorb a temporary coordination gap. There is no recurring contract, no control of the goods margin and no proprietary certification. The founder ends with an intermittent expert practice. Better software cannot repair the missing willingness to pay.

The first ninety days

  1. Days 1–15: retain one Dutch-speaking construction-quality consultant for a €1,000 fee hurdle. Review the service boundary, one intake sheet and one exception report. The consultant must remain independent of any quality assurer engaged on the same project.
  2. Days 1–25: ask two quality-assurer firms to review ten recent Polish-origin joinery or steel orders each, anonymised. Count complete-on-first-review files, document requests, site deviations, lost hours and effects on the assurer’s statement. If they cannot identify origin reliably, record that the pitch’s first number cannot be measured this way.
  3. Days 10–35: interview four direct-import builders and four Polish factories. Reconstruct the last order from quotation through installation. Ask who supplied each declaration and instruction, what was rejected, who repaired it and whether any fee was visible.
  4. Days 25–50: send one scoped €2,000 pre-order-gate offer, 50% in advance, to ten qualified builders. Cap Dutch support, travel, insurance advice and document handling at €3,500 before payment. Build no portal, buy no goods and sign no factory exclusivity.
  5. Days 40–75: deliver one paid gate beside the day job. Time supplier chasing, technical judgement, Dutch review and rework separately. Offer the same buyer the €3,000 completion pack before installation; never rebate it through a commission.
  6. Days 76–90: pass only if one of ten builders pays for the gate, buys the completion pack, and the two stages fit within forty-eight delivery hours. Also require the quality-assurer sample to show at least four material first-review gaps in twenty Polish-origin orders. These are test hurdles, not observed rates. Kill if eighteen of twenty files were good enough on first review, no builder pays after ten qualified offers, or Dutch review pushes expected gross margin below 50%.

Verdict

WATCH

The rule creates evidence work, but official guidance and incumbent behaviour both suggest that much of it is already bundled with the product or the builder’s normal process. The single assumption that would most change the verdict is that a direct-import builder will pay the separate €5,000-per-order hurdle for acceptance evidence, without hiding the fee in a goods commission.
