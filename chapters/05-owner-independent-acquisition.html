Essay 5 of 6 · Owner-independent B2B acquisition · from pitch 3

Buy the handover, not the owner's job

The purchase is not a cheap Dutch company. It is cash flow that still exists after the seller leaves, the Dutch account owner stays and acquisition debt is paid. A specialist B2B distributor can fit the reader's capital, languages and engineering habit. It also converts a day job into a leveraged operating job on closing day. The fatal case is simple: “profit” was the retiring owner's unpaid sales labour, customers followed that person, and inventory absorbed the remaining cash. Test the supply edge before searching. Then reject every deal that cannot pay a replacement manager and debt under a flat-sales downside.

The proposition

The buyer is the reader. The purchase event is a retiring founder deciding that family, staff or a strategic buyer will not take the firm. The target is a Dutch specialist distributor of physical B2B consumables or replacement parts. It has two to ten staff, repeat orders, no product requiring software support and no single qualification held only by the seller.

The deliverable is a going concern plus a signed transfer file. That file contains five years of reconciled accounts, customer and supplier concentration, payroll, an owner-task diary, stock ageing, the normal working-capital level, maintenance needs and a debt-service downside. It also names the Dutch account owner who will remain after control changes.

The boundary matters. Do not buy a general webshop, a fashion brand, a regulated product whose licence leaves with one person, or a distributor whose largest account deals only with the seller. Do not buy hoped-for Polish sourcing. A new CEE supply line is upside and must carry no purchase price. This remains a physical trading company. Better quoting and inventory controls can be internal tools, but customers never receive a product with a login.

The substitute is to keep the developer job and invest the capital. For the seller, substitutes are a family transfer, employee buy-out, strategic sale or orderly closure. For the reader, building a distributor from zero avoids inherited liabilities but must create supplier credit, stock, fulfilment and customer trust. Buying is justified only when those assets transfer at less than their cost and the team already runs ordinary days without the founder.

What changed

There is no new rule and no compliance deadline. No owner is obliged to sell, no lender is obliged to finance, and no customer has budget because succession exists. The clock is demographic and voluntary. In a December 2025 ABN AMRO/Ipsos I&O survey of 519 entrepreneurs, 68% expected to stop steering within ten years. Among that group, 22% had not started succession and 20% were searching without a successor.

Wholesale is relevant, but not automatically attractive. ABN's CBS-based table counted 5,210 owners aged 65 or older in wholesale and trade intermediation, or 15.7% of owners of firms with at least two working people. Brookz displayed 95 wholesale businesses for sale when checked. That is seller behaviour on one marketplace, not completed deals or evidence that any listing fits this buy box.

Demand for the acquisition therefore means a seller opening records, accepting a transition and negotiating risk allocation. The ABN survey supports a future supply of conversations. The marketplace supports present availability. Neither proves transferable cash flow. The budget appears only when a lender underwrites one target and the seller leaves money contingent or subordinated.

Sector averages provide a warning rather than a valuation. CBS reported 2024 wholesale revenue of €648.435 billion and pre-tax result of €34.481 billion, a 5.3% aggregate margin. The series covers all sizes, so large firms dominate it; it is not a small-target margin. A buyer who needs a heroic margin expansion to service debt has not found an owner-independent company. They have financed a turnaround.

Why you

The useful edges are narrower than “developer buys boring business.” English, Polish and Russian help qualify CEE suppliers and speak directly with parts makers. Regulatory literacy helps reject products with hidden importer duties. An engineering habit helps expose stock, quote, picking and quality failures as measurable work. The audience can produce introductions to industrial buyers and operators. None of those edges proves that this reader can retain a Dutch customer book.

The Dutch-language gate is commercial. A2 is not enough for an angry local buyer, a performance conversation with an employee or a subtle price negotiation. The gate passes only if a Dutch account owner remains under a signed employment and retention arrangement, leads the top-customer handover and already owns routine relationships. It fails if “the team speaks Dutch” but every exception still goes to the seller. The buyer should attend those calls, improve Dutch and remain accountable. Translation is not customer ownership.

Hammond Chemicals is a useful precedent elsewhere. According to lender Shawbrook's case study, Euston Ventures acquired the UK blender, packager and distributor of industrial solvents. Jim Ward became chief executive, third-generation family members remained in leadership, and Shawbrook supported the transaction. The independent Companies House trace lists Hammond Chemicals Limited as active, incorporated in 1969 and engaged in wholesale of chemical products. Together, the sources illustrate a CEO change with family leaders retained. They do not independently verify the transaction structure.

The sources used here publish no transaction price or target cash flow. They therefore support no comparable multiple. Copy only the retained-leadership pattern. The personal constraint is equally sharp: search can run beside the job, but operation cannot. On closing day the software job ends. If employment must continue, do not sign.

The first sale

Before becoming a buyer, sell one cross-border order through an incumbent distributor. The ten prospect types are distributors of industrial fasteners, bearings and seals, workshop consumables, packaging materials, industrial hose, workwear, cleaning chemistry, adhesives, water-treatment spares and material-handling parts. Each must serve business customers, carry stock or coordinate fulfilment, and have a Dutch commercial lead.

The offer is a €750 fixed-fee sourcing diagnostic, a price hurdle rather than an observed rate. It covers one named item family, three qualified CEE suppliers, landed-cost comparison, sample coordination and one buyer-approved order route. The existing distributor remains importer and seller of record. It pays suppliers, sets the customer price and accepts the product. The reader supplies no legal opinion and touches no regulated category without specialist review.

The signer is the distributor owner or purchasing manager. The purchase event is a stock-out, supplier failure, margin review or customer request that the current range cannot satisfy. A generic promise of cheaper Polish goods is not enough. The diagnostic wins only when the buyer gives a real specification and pays before samples are ordered.

Start in Noord-Holland and the Randstad, using English with export-facing distributors and Polish with suppliers. Use a Dutch commercial freelancer only when a prospect requests Dutch, at a fixed customer-funded cost with no share of the later acquisition. Ten properly scoped offers are enough for the first test. If every serious buyer requires native-Dutch selling from the founder, this channel fails before a broker receives a fee.

The acquisition sale begins only after that order. Send a one-page buy box to accountants, BuyInside, Brookz advisers and owners in the ten categories. Ask for three facts before signing an NDA: staff excluding the seller, percentage of gross profit from the largest five customers, and who approves exceptions when the seller is absent. A listing without those answers is a lead, not an opportunity.

Does the money work?

The number to settle first was acquisition finance for a first-time buyer with €100,000. The public part is now clear. Qredits publishes an MKB loan of €50,000–€250,000, 9.95% annual interest, a 1.5% fee, monthly linear or annuity payments, an average five-year term and a maximum ten-year term. It expressly includes company acquisition as a use.

The missing part remains decisive. Qredits says it assesses the completed application, plan and financial information after a personal conversation. The reviewed public record does not settle an approval formula for this buyer or an acquisition-specific ten-year promise. BMKB does not settle that gap. RVO says a starter is under three years, a starter loan can be at most €333,333, and the state guarantee is 67.5%. The scheme enlarges collateral; it is not a loan offer.

Qredits' currently linked MKB conditions add the critical term. All business assets serve as collateral. When the borrower is a legal person, Qredits asks its directors to sign a guarantee for joint liability. A full €160,000 personal guarantee would put €210,000 at risk after the €50,000 cash commitment. That fails the confirmed risk cap. The Qredits route survives only if the signed offer limits total personal recourse to the remaining headroom.

The actual bank rate, guarantee fee and permitted seller-note terms remain unavailable without a target and lender decision. The amount of personal recourse in a specific Qredits offer is also unavailable. Therefore the model uses Qredits' published rate and five-year amortisation only as a debt-service stress. It assumes no lender approval. A written indicative response to this model is a ninety-day pass condition.

Every amount below except the Qredits product terms is a design hurdle, not a market price or forecast. The purchase-price hurdle is €300,000: €40,000 buyer cash, a €160,000 senior loan and a €100,000 seller note. The note is subordinated during the senior loan, carries 5% cash interest and then repays in five annual €20,000 instalments. Those invented terms require written lender and seller acceptance.

Lender fees, diligence and transaction work have a separate €10,000 hurdle. The ninety-day budget comes from that allowance, rather than sitting above it. Cash committed remains €50,000, and no further personal recourse is allowed. KVK describes stacked acquisition finance and illustrates seller-subordinated funding repaid after bank debt.

The €40,747 annual senior-debt stress is arithmetic from Qredits' published rate and a 60-month annuity, not a lender quote. The €90,000 CEO line is total employer cost. It comprises €72,000 gross salary and €18,000 employer tax, pension and benefit overhead. The owner receives only the salary before personal tax.

The explicit owner-income target is the €72,000 gross salary. It is a design hurdle, not the reader's reported salary. The model counts no dividend as owner income. The remaining €30,300 is company cash before corporate tax, legal reserves and any distribution. Tax advice must settle what may eventually leave the company.

During years two through five, the senior annuity and €5,000 seller interest leave €74,300 of company cash before corporate tax. During years six through ten, five €20,000 principal instalments retire the seller note. Interest falls from €5,000 to €1,000 on opening principal. That phase leaves €95,000 to €99,000 before corporate tax and distributions. Principal is now a cash claim, not owner wealth.

Even if operations stay at the year-one hurdle, the seller phase leaves €51,000 to €55,000 after note service and the €20,000 working-capital reserve. That is downside arithmetic, not a forecast. The salary target was already charged above it.

The target must arrive with normal inventory and receivables, less normal payables, at closing. Otherwise the working-capital peg reduces the price euro for euro. Growth is not free. Both later phases retain €30,000 for added stock and customer credit. Any forecast that counts higher sales but not the cash trapped in them is rejected.

The kill fact cannot be settled from listings, CBS or lender pages. Only target payroll, bank statements, VAT returns, invoices, stock ageing and an observed owner-task log can show replacement-adjusted cash flow. KVK says due diligence must test the seller's information, figures, contracts, staff hours and salaries, and asks whether customers will deal with the buyer. Require at least €96,000 annual cash before acquisition debt under flat sales, after the €90,000 CEO replacement line. Below that, or if a 10% gross-profit fall breaks debt service, stop.

From service to company

Customer one after closing is still a transfer exercise. The Dutch account owner leads; the buyer observes the order, exception, picking, delivery and complaint path. Every promise made by the seller becomes a written owner, limit or contract term. The first accumulating asset is a clean map from customer and item to gross profit, working capital and service failure.

Customer five proves that the firm can win without the retired founder. Quotes use a landed-cost template. Supplier qualification records specification, sample approval, product duties, lead time and defect response. Warehouse and commercial staff can see the same reorder and exception rules. The buyer handles capital allocation and supplier negotiation, not every quote.

Customer twenty arrives through a repeatable niche proposition, not the reader's LinkedIn personality. The account owner sells in Dutch; the buyer opens CEE supply and manages cash. Routine purchasing, replenishment, quote preparation and invoice matching are delegated. Quality release, large credit decisions, key hires and supplier disputes remain human.

The twentieth delivery should need fewer founder minutes than the first because item data, approved suppliers, packaging instructions and failure history already exist. The asset is the qualified range, reorder history, customer trust, supplier credit and trained team. Internal automation may reduce clerical work. It cannot unload a pallet, inspect a sample, renegotiate a damaged order or reassure a customer after control changes.

The founder bottleneck is not code. It is saying yes to too many low-margin items, extending credit and becoming the new personal account owner. Measure founder-touched quotes, exceptions and customer calls each week. If those counts do not fall after six months, the acquisition bought another owner-dependent job.

The incumbent gets a turn

The reader is not the only disciplined outsider. BuyInside markets a Dutch service devoted to management buy-ins, including candidate profiles, search, transaction advice and a platform that introduces external successors to sellers and investors. It says it has focused on MBI succession for fifteen years. Brookz already exposes wholesale listings to buyers. These firms have Dutch fluency, adviser relationships and buyers who may look safer than a developer changing careers.

The seller's best substitute may be internal. A commercial manager knows the accounts and can pair with an investor. A supplier, customer or adjacent distributor can pay more because it removes duplicated warehouse, purchasing or administration. A family successor can accept a slower transfer. The reader should not “win” an auction by turning every synergy available only to a strategic buyer into price.

Brookz's H1-2026 adviser survey covered 291 Dutch M&A advisers serving firms with €0.5–50 million revenue. It reported a 3.6 EBITDA multiple for firms at €200,000 normalised EBITDA. It also reported that 19% of processes broke over valuation gaps and named owner dependence and customer spread as valuation factors. This is a market cross-check, not a bid formula. The proposed target is smaller, and its relevant earnings are after replacement labour.

Margin can be captured from four directions. The seller can demand payment for personal goodwill. The lender receives cash before the owner. The retained account manager can demand a change-of-control premium. Customers can use the handover to reopen price. The defence is not a clever model. It is contingent consideration, direct customer reference calls, staff retention terms and a walk-away price.

The capable failure

Assume the buyer executes well. The Dutch account owner stays. Customers remain. A Polish supplier passes samples and gives a lower unit cost. Sales rise. The company still fails its owner.

The new line needs larger minimum orders and eight weeks of stock. Two major customers take longer payment terms in exchange for volume. Gross profit rises on paper while cash is locked in inventory and receivables. Senior-debt amortisation continues monthly. The seller note remains due, and the retained manager now has credible leverage over compensation.

No fraud, bad selling or poor implementation caused this. The acquisition structure assigned fixed claims to lender and seller while growth made the owner fund more working capital. Refusing growth protects cash but removes the reason to pay for the CEE edge. Taking growth requires fresh equity and dilutes the buyer. Capable execution loses because the capital cycle, not the gross margin, owns the company.

The first ninety days

  1. Days 1–10: write the one-page buy box and downside model. Send the anonymous €300,000 structure to Qredits and two lenders using BMKB. Ask for written indicative term, security, guarantee-fee and personal-guarantee ranges. Spend nothing.
  2. Days 1–20: interview ten distributor owners, purchasing managers or account leads across the named categories. Ask who owns the top relationships, what a stock-out costs and where CEE supply has failed. Budget a €600 travel and Dutch-language hurdle.
  3. Days 15–45: offer ten €750 sourcing diagnostics, paid in advance. Use existing distributors as importer and seller. Cap samples, translation and supplier checks at €1,200. Do this beside the day job, with no customer promise during employed hours.
  4. Days 30–60: give the buy box to Brookz advisers, BuyInside and five accountants. Request three anonymised information memoranda. Pay up to €1,500 for an independent accountant to build one normalisation workbook. Do not sign exclusivity.
  5. Days 55–80: apply the workbook to three targets. Replace the seller's hours at the €90,000 CEO hurdle, stress gross profit by 10%, map working capital and call no customers without permission. Reject any file that needs post-close “synergy” to pay debt.
  6. Days 75–90: spend up to €1,500 on Dutch acquisition counsel only if a target and lender both pass. Draft heads for account-owner retention, working-capital peg, seller subordination and customer-reference access. Total pre-evidence spend is capped at €4,800. Every euro is debited from the €10,000 non-equity allowance, leaving at most €5,200 for later lender, diligence and closing costs.

Pass: one distributor pays €750, and a lender gives a written €160,000 path that keeps total buyer cash and personal recourse within €50,000. One seller must also open records showing at least €96,000 cash before acquisition debt after replacement labour. The flat-sales model must cover senior debt at least 1.5 times, a hurdle rather than an observed covenant. Kill: no paid diagnostic after ten qualified offers; every lender breaks the €50,000 total-risk cap; or all three targets depend on the seller for a key account, qualification or ordinary decision. Passing authorises diligence, not a bid. Closing ends the day job.

Verdict

ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD

The path buys supplier credit, stock routines, staff and customer trust that a new distributor would have to create. It is step two, not side income: search beside the job, then leave the job at closing. The single assumption that would most change the verdict is that a qualifying target exists. It must show €96,000 of annual cash before acquisition debt after replacement-cost leadership and accept financing that caps all buyer exposure at €50,000. If no target does, keep the paid sourcing service or stop.
