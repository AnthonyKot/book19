Essay 2 of 6 · Border files for CEE imports · from pitch 7

The border creates the file. The broker owns the border.

A Dutch steel importer can clear its next load only if the right declarant sits behind the customs entry. Its later carbon bill depends on data held by the foreign mill. That gap appears made for a regulatory reader who can call the supplier in its own language. The fatal point is control. The importer or customs representative keeps the legal role, while a broker can bundle the same data chase into clearance. The first purchase is therefore not a grand compliance retainer. It is one paid supplier-evidence diagnostic, sold through a Dutch declarant, which proves that language can recover better data than the default-value route.

The proposition

The first buyer is the finance, procurement or customs lead of a Dutch metal importer above the CBAM threshold. It buys steel or aluminium from one Ukrainian, Turkish or Kazakh installation. The purchase event is a stopped import, an authorisation review, or the decision between default and actual emissions before the first annual declaration.

The initial unit is one importer, one producing installation and one customs-code family. The fixed product is a supplier-evidence diagnostic. It reconciles customs quantities, identifies the declared production route, obtains the supplier's available monitoring record, maps missing evidence, and compares the two lawful reporting routes. It ends with a handover pack for the importer or its authorised customs representative.

The business does not clear goods, become importer of record, buy certificates, verify emissions or give legal advice. A verifier must verify actual emissions. The authorised declarant remains responsible for the declaration. The entrant coordinates the foreign installation and prepares evidence for those accountable parties. It never becomes software with a login.

The substitute is strong: use Commission default values, let procurement email the supplier, and have the existing customs broker submit the file. Default values require no verified installation report, although the Dutch authority says they usually create higher CBAM costs. The service wins only where the avoidable certificate cost and internal effort exceed its fee.

The wood file is a later product, not part of the first sale. It would map Ukrainian timber origin, geolocation and legality evidence for the Dutch operator, then prepare a due-diligence statement for an authorised representative to submit. It earns a place only after a timber importer pays for a diagnostic. Combining two acronyms before either buyer pays would hide two sales motions.

What changed

CBAM is no longer a coming rule. Its definitive regime began on 1 January 2026. The Dutch threshold is more than 50 tonnes per importer per calendar year across covered goods, excluding electricity and hydrogen. Once crossed, the obligations apply to all covered imports in that year. The covered physical categories include iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen.

An EU importer above the threshold needs authorised-declarant status, unless an indirect customs representative agrees to take over the CBAM obligations. Customs checks the status at import and does not release the goods without it. Applications filed by 31 March 2026 received a temporary pending-decision route. That date has passed. A later applicant that crosses the threshold must wait for approval, for which Customs states a maximum 120-day treatment period.

The recurring event sits later. The first annual declaration covers 2026 and is due by 30 September 2027. The obligation exists now, while much of the cash and filing work arrives next year. Enforcement creates urgency for authorisation. It does not prove a 2026 consulting budget for supplier data.

The actual-data route is narrower than the pitch implied. A producing installation prepares an emissions report, and an accredited CBAM verifier checks it. The entrant can organise inputs but cannot turn unverified mill figures into actual values. In July 2026 the NEa said no accredited CBAM verifiers were yet available; nine firms had applied to the Dutch accreditation council and about thirty more had shown interest. Default values therefore remain both a substitute and a bridge.

The rule nevertheless puts a price on better evidence. Standard values require no verified installation report. They receive an uplift of 10% for 2026, 20% for 2027 and 30% from 2028; the NEa says they usually produce higher costs than actual data. That difference is the budget mechanism. It still must be calculated for one importer's codes, origin, quantity and production route before anyone quotes a service.

The pitch's number cannot be settled as asked. A March 2026 NEa newsletter reported almost 1,100 applications, 870 approved, and about 250 new applications after the definitive period began. It also said increasing numbers of applicants were below 50 tonnes and needed no authorisation. An earlier NEa implementation estimate put the post-threshold Dutch population at no more than 1,500 declarants, based on 2023–2025 import statistics.

Those records do not identify how many applicants were above 50 tonnes, how many filed before 31 March, or how many eligible importers missed the date. The requested numerator and denominator are not public. The honest addressable record is “no more than 1,500” plus an application count contaminated by unnecessary filings. That is enough to reject market-size theatre, not enough to forecast customers.

EUDR moved the other way. An enacted December 2025 amendment delayed general application to 30 December 2026 and most micro or small operators to 30 June 2027. It also reduced repeated downstream submissions by concentrating the due-diligence statement on the first operator placing the product on the EU market. The deadline has slipped twice, so budget timing deserves skepticism.

Ukraine is classified low-risk. Low-risk sourcing permits simplified due diligence when origin is unmixed, though the operator must still collect compliance information. More damaging to the original pitch, Ukraine's State Forest Agency says its tracking system already follows batches from harvest to export and provides legality documents and geolocation tools. The file may still need assembly in the EU system. The claim that polygons must be extracted by phone is no longer credible.

Why you

The language edge is real only at the foreign installation. The useful call is not “please complete this questionnaire.” It is a conversation with the energy engineer about production routes, precursors, metering periods and why a number will face verification. Russian can work in parts of Ukraine and Kazakhstan; Polish helps with regional suppliers and associates. English connects the result to the importer's finance and customs teams.

Regulatory literacy supplies the boundary. It separates threshold, authorisation, actual values, default values, verification and certificate cash. The engineering habit turns an email chase into a data lineage: quantity to customs entry, good to code, product to installation, installation to process, process to source record. Internal automation can reduce errors, but the customer buys a signed handover pack, not access to a tool.

The Dutch-language gate is conditional, not passed. No Dutch trade exam appears in the CBAM roles reviewed here; the legal roles are importer, authorised declarant, indirect representative and accredited verifier. The operator's technical emissions report must be submitted in English under the implementing regulation. That helps with the mill. It does not make A2 sufficient for Dutch cold sales, customs disputes or a finance director's risk questions.

The answer is a Dutch customs partner. The partner owns the local relationship, customs representation and Dutch explanation. The entrant contracts as its named CEE supplier-data specialist. Customs confirms that a service party may deliver the CBAM filing on the importer's behalf through chain authorisation, while the importer remains liable. If the partner insists on owning the method, supplier relationship and all margin, the channel fails.

Ingdilligenz GmbH in Würzburg is the precedent. Its own site markets import-data analysis, supplier communication, emissions support, XML preparation and report submission as a CBAM representative. An independent register trace records HRB 15952, incorporation on 17 November 2021 and €25,000 share capital. This proves that a small firm can persist on the shape. It does not establish Dutch prices, independence from software or payment for the language layer.

The constraint that breaks the fit is the buyer side. If the reader cannot find a Dutch broker willing to lead meetings and retain the importer contract, A2 makes direct acquisition brittle. If the reader wants physical site work, this also fails personally. It is desk work attached to metal, and the least physical candidate in the book.

The first sale

Build ten prospect types, not a list of every importer. Use Ukrainian-steel stockholders, Turkish-steel fabricators, aluminium-profile importers, façade-system suppliers, fastening wholesalers, trailer and body builders, greenhouse-structure suppliers, metal furniture makers, fertiliser wholesalers and precast or cement-product importers. Each prospect must have a Dutch entity, a known non-EU supplier, likely annual mass above the threshold and no visible carbon team.

The route begins with two Dutch customs brokers and one carbon-accounting adviser. Ask each for one anonymised client whose supplier data remain incomplete. The partner should introduce the diagnostic, lead Dutch calls and keep declaration responsibility where already contracted. Start in the Zaandam–Rotterdam logistics corridor, but follow the supplier relationship rather than a postcode.

The fixed offer is a €2,000 design hurdle, 50% in advance. It covers one importer, one installation and one product family. The output is an actual-versus-default cost map, supplier evidence inventory, structured request, two foreign-language supplier sessions and a handover memo. It excludes verification, legal advice, customs correction and final filing.

The finance or customs lead signs. A stopped shipment is the urgent event; otherwise the decision is whether better data can save more certificate cash than it costs to obtain and verify. The diagnostic must state that saving before proposing a retainer. If default values are cheaper than the full data-and-verification path, recommend the default route and stop.

The kill fact is only partly settled. Customs Support Group markets customs clearance beside CBAM and EUDR reporting, with more than 1,700 customs experts and 60,000 clients. Deloitte Netherlands markets an integrated service spanning supplier solicitation, validation, authorised declaration and customs brokerage. These firms can bundle the work. Neither publishes a comparable small-importer price or says the supplier-data work is free. Two live broker quotes are still required.

Does the money work?

Every amount below is a labelled design hurdle, not a market price or forecast. Certificates, verifier fees, customs duties and legal work remain on the customer's invoice. Passing them through would inflate revenue and create working-capital risk without adding margin.

Year one is paid discovery. Four diagnostics and two small retainers can fit around a job if supplier calls are scheduled and the broker handles emergencies. It cannot replace developer income. The negative result is deliberate: founder time is not free merely because it is used at night.

The mature case clears the owner target, but only at recurring prices far above the pitch's original per-shipment idea. A €150 statement fee cannot support senior supplier investigation. The mature CBAM retainer must cover a defined importer portfolio and leave verification outside. The EUDR retainer is lower because Ukraine's low-risk status and state tools reduce work.

Capacity is the real model. Twenty CBAM clients at one delivery day a month consume 240 days a year. Fifteen EUDR clients at half a day consume another 90. Supplier onboardings add about 45 days. One analyst and one associate provide most routine capacity; the founder sells, qualifies, escalates and reviews. If each mill still consumes founder negotiation every quarter, the table has too few people.

Working capital should be almost absent. Bill diagnostics 50% in advance and retainers quarterly in advance. Do not hold certificates or import goods. The year-one cash-at-risk hurdle is €12,000, released in stages after payment. Before evidence, spend at most €4,000. That is well below the €50,000 rule and leaves the reader's capital outside this experiment.

From service to company

Customer one is founder work. The asset is a controlled lineage for one installation: customs code, quantity, product, route, input, emissions source, responsible person, verifier question and unresolved exception. Every value keeps its origin and date. The final pack can leave as spreadsheets and documents.

Customer five should include a second customs partner and a repeated producing installation. A trained CEE associate runs supplier intake and checks completeness. A carbon specialist reviews method questions. The founder handles qualification and exceptions. At least half the delivery hours must occur without the founder.

Customer twenty means twenty importers across at least three channels. The twentieth receives the same scope, request protocol, evidence schema and default-versus-actual decision tree. The accumulating asset is a permissioned supplier and installation library, plus known evidence failures by production route. It is not a portal sold to customers.

Re-use needs consent. A mill can register its verified report so authorised declarants can use its data. Contract and confidentiality terms still decide whether the entrant can retain a copy. Store only what the importer and installation permit. If every pack is customer-confidential and every supplier starts from zero, the asset is merely the team's experience.

Human work remains at the boundary: persuading the mill, understanding how production is measured, spotting an implausible mapping and deciding when to involve the verifier. Data transfer, evidence indexing, reconciliation and routine supplier follow-up can be delegated. The founder bottleneck is trusted multilingual escalation. The twentieth delivery is different only if prior installation knowledge cuts those hours.

The incumbent gets a turn

The customs broker begins with the account, import data, authorisation and border workflow. Customs Support Group explicitly places CBAM and EUDR inside a portfolio of customs clearance, reporting and outsourcing. Deloitte can join supplier data, declaration, certificates and brokerage in one managed service. The customer may prefer one accountable counterparty even if its foreign-language follow-up is slower.

The free substitute is also improving. The CBAM registry carries default values, while the Dutch authority publishes a calculator and guidance. A large mill can register its installation once and share a verified record with buyers. A procurement employee can then connect it to customs quantities. Better standards and tooling compress the mechanical work.

On wood, the Ukrainian state is building the entrant's proposed upstream data service. Its tools expose origin, harvesting documents and geolocation. EU reform reduced downstream repetition, and low-risk classification removes full risk assessment in qualifying cases. The wood line should be a cheap readiness diagnostic, not a bet on manual polygon retrieval.

The partner-capture response is structural. A broker gives the entrant two difficult suppliers, observes the protocol, then hires a Russian-speaking coordinator or sends the rest to Deloitte's tooling. The entrant never owned authorisation or the importer. Its defence is a cross-broker installation library and faster CEE resolution. Both depend on permissions the partner may refuse.

The capable failure

The company executes well. It recovers usable data from several mills, hands clean packs to Dutch brokers and helps importers compare actual and default costs. Accredited verifiers arrive. The largest mills then register their reports centrally, making the same verified dataset available to every customer.

Meanwhile the brokers add one multilingual coordinator and standardise their intake. Default values remain acceptable for small or dirty portfolios. For clean portfolios, supplier data become reusable and require little chasing. The valuable transition work trains both sides to remove the intermediary.

Nothing failed through poor execution. The legal role always belonged to the declarant, the source record belonged to the mill, and the customer belonged to the broker. The entrant kept no exclusive asset. Revenue becomes occasional remediation for strange installations. That can pay expert day rates, but it cannot sustain the recurring client count or founder replacement in the mature table.

The first ninety days

  1. Days 1–15: interview two Dutch customs brokers, one carbon accountant and six importers from the ten prospect types. Reconstruct one 2026 entry, the authorisation holder, supplier contact, chosen data route and any fee already paid. Conduct meetings outside day-job hours.
  2. Days 10–25: have a CBAM specialist review the scope, disclaimer and actual-versus-default worksheet. Spend at most a €1,500 hurdle. Do not market verification or declarant status.
  3. Days 20–40: select one product family and create a bilingual supplier request, evidence index and handover pack. Use the customer's existing files. Cap translation, secure storage and professional-insurance advice at a further €1,500 hurdle.
  4. Days 30–60: offer the €2,000 diagnostic to five qualified importers through at least two brokers. Require one named installation, 2026 customs quantities, a responsible declarant and a supplier contact. Take 50% in advance.
  5. Days 45–75: deliver one paid diagnostic. Compare the total cost of default values with actual data, including the customer's verifier quote. Record founder hours, associate hours, supplier responses and which evidence survives specialist review.
  6. Days 76–90: ask for a six-month €750 monthly retainer and a second importer from a different broker. In parallel, show one Ukrainian timber importer a €2,000 EUDR readiness diagnostic, but build nothing until paid. Total pre-evidence spend is capped at €4,000.

Pass: one CBAM diagnostic is paid, its buyer documents a net saving or avoided internal cost above the fee, and one broker offers a second importer while accepting the service boundary and supplier-data rights. Kill: no payment after five qualified offers; two brokers include comparable supplier work at no incremental charge; every case prefers default values after verification cost; or the partner will not let the entrant retain a reusable contact and process asset. The public record shows provider scope, not prices, and does not prove Dutch SME payment.

Verdict

TEST THROUGH A PARTNER

The single assumption that would most change the verdict is that a Dutch customs broker will introduce a paying importer while leaving the entrant enough fee and permission to build a reusable CEE supplier-data asset.
