Essay 4 of 6 · The spray crew's file · from pitch 10

The limit moved. The hygienist owns the gate.

A contractor sprays polyurethane foam or two-part coating. The Dutch exposure limits changed in April 2026, while the crew's training records have already been mandatory for three years. The attractive product is one crew, one observed process and one defensible file. The fatal detail is that a model may finish the assessment without measurement, and the reader is not an occupational hygienist. The purchase decision is therefore narrow: will a small contractor pay for a partner-signed assessment and a bilingual operating record, rather than use its existing arbodienst, supplier course and prevention employee?

The proposition

The first buyer is the owner or operations manager of a Dutch contractor with five to fifty workers and at least one recurring spray process. The first targets are spray-foam insulation, floor coating and vehicle refinishing. The purchase event is a new product, booth, crew or method; an RI&E review; a principal-contractor questionnaire; or an inspection that exposes a missing assessment.

The unit is one crew doing one defined process under one set of controls. The fixed-scope spray-crew baseline inventories products and safety data sheets, maps tasks and workers, audits training evidence, observes a normal job, and produces a quantitative exposure assessment. The Dutch-speaking occupational hygienist chooses and reviews the method. If a model cannot close the question, the file specifies representative measurements and records the results.

The customer receives the assessment report, training register, action list and explicit review triggers. A trigger is a changed chemical, application method, ventilation arrangement, shift pattern or crew. The offer does not promise an annual measurement because the reviewed rules create no such calendar. It sells a current file and separately priced change reviews.

The business refuses to be an arbodienst, medical provider, laboratory, training platform or respirator dealer. It does not diagnose workers or declare a workplace safe. Laboratories and occupational physicians contract directly with the employer when needed. It never becomes software with a login. Its product is an observed process, controlled evidence and a report the employer can own.

The substitute is strong: the employer's prevention employee can use a recognised model, retain its present arbodienst, buy a measurement study from SGS Search, or ask a broad safety consultancy to update the RI&E. SGS markets the instruments, laboratory analysis, interpretation and report in one service. The entrant wins only if crew access, speed and language reduce senior hygienist time enough to leave a margin.

What changed

The Dutch implementation is enacted, not proposed. Staatsblad 2025-337 took effect on 9 April 2026. Until 31 December 2028, the diisocyanate limits are 10 micrograms NCO per cubic metre over the working period and 20 for short exposure. From 1 January 2029 they become 6 and 12. The transition gives employers time to change processes and controls; it does not postpone the current 10/20 limits.

Government guidance tells employers to put lead and diisocyanate risks in the RI&E, add suitable measures to its action plan, offer medical controls where required and give specific information to workers. Those are employer duties. They establish work, not a budget for this entrant, and the employer remains responsible after buying a report.

A separate, older rule creates the training record. REACH entry 74 has barred industrial or professional use above 0.1% diisocyanates since 24 August 2023 unless the employer or self-employed user ensures successful training before use. Training must match the process: ventilated-booth spraying is intermediate, while open-air or high-energy spraying such as foam is advanced. The employer documents completion and renews training at least every five years. Supplier material must be available in the official language of the Member State.

That makes a certificate audit necessary but cheap to copy. It is not the moat. The harder record is the site-specific exposure assessment. The Labour Inspectorate says a basic characterisation may use a model. Its result can close the assessment or lead to measurements, and the chosen approach and results must be recorded in a report.

This tests the pitch's first kill fact. A quantitative model is accepted; measurement is not automatic. The Inspectorate also says measurement may need several days because conditions vary, and that the assessment must be performed by someone at occupational-hygienist level. The viable unit is therefore not “a pump for every crew.” It is expert triage first, measurement only where the method and uncertainty require it.

Enforcement can create the buying event. In an earlier inspection programme covering composite businesses, inspectors found violations at 76% of 89 initial visits and 42% of 67 follow-ups. They issued warnings, requirements and seven fines; four later fines concerned failure to establish or assess exposure. Inspectors also recorded firms seeking expert help after inspection. This is adjacent evidence, not a 2026 survey of spray contractors. It shows enforcement changing behaviour, but it shows no invoice or willingness to pay the proposed price.

The pitch's number to settle first cannot be settled from the public record. In 2020 the Health Council said there was no register of PUR-foam installers and cited only a 2019 estimate of 100 to 200 individual workers. The current NVPU member page lists two organisations under “Sprayers,” but it is a membership directory, not a census. Vehicle repair, industrial coating and other spray users sit in different activity codes, while REACH requires each employer to keep its own completions. No public source found here reports either the national firm total or the share with valid certificates.

Why you

The engineering habit fits the work. A defensible assessment connects product composition, task, duration, ventilation, worker behaviour, model boundary, measurement and control. Regulatory literacy keeps the REACH training duty separate from the Dutch exposure limit. The reader can build the chain and refuse a template that merely copies a safety data sheet.

Polish and Russian help where the assessment depends on what workers actually do: how they mix, spray, clean, remove protection and respond to a blocked hose. The reader's CEE network can reach subcontracted crews and foreign-owned shops that a national provider may serve awkwardly. Those are working hypotheses from the edge inventory, not evidence that most Dutch spray crews speak these languages.

The Dutch gate is real twice. First, the employer, RI&E owner and Inspectorate operate in Dutch. A2 is not enough to discover commercial ambiguity or defend a technical report. Second, the Inspectorate expects occupational-hygienist-level expertise. Its page names a competence level, not a statutory licence or an automatic right for this reader to sign.

The honest answer is a partner. A Dutch-speaking occupational hygienist qualifies the job, selects the model or sampling strategy, reviews interpretation and signs the professional report from the first sale. The reader manages evidence, observes the site and conducts supplementary Polish or Russian interviews. Dutch training material remains the supplier's duty under REACH; a crew-language explanation cannot replace the required expert course. If the partner will not accept that division, the reader has no direct route.

The precedent is Synergy Environmental Solutions in Stoke-on-Trent. Its occupational-hygiene arm markets air and biological isocyanate monitoring for vehicle refinishing, PU foam, adhesive spraying, composites and industrial coating. Companies House independently records company 07937140 as active, incorporated on 6 February 2012, with environmental consulting among its activities and accounts through March 2025. That proves the service shape can persist elsewhere. It does not transfer British rules, prices or customer behaviour to the Netherlands.

The constraint that breaks the fit is availability for real site work. Spraying cannot be reconstructed from a weekend spreadsheet, and a representative job may move at short notice. The reader needs reserved weekdays beside the day job and a partner who can attend or accept controlled fieldwork. A two-year qualification route would change the economics, but it violates the agreed retraining constraint.

The first sale

Build ten prospect types, then qualify the chemical and task rather than assuming exposure from a trade label. Screen spray-foam insulation contractors, PU-floor installers, industrial protective-coating firms, car body shops and commercial-vehicle refinishers. Add rail-coating contractors, yacht and ship painters, composite fabricators, spray-adhesive users and PU-elastomer processors. Exclude firms whose safety data sheets stay below the REACH threshold or whose work never creates the defined spray exposure.

Reach them through two independent Dutch hygienists, coating and foam suppliers, training providers, industry associations and direct English or Polish notes. A supplier introduction is useful but dangerous because the supplier may own both the course and customer. The first conversation reconstructs the last product change, inspection, exposure assessment and training audit. Ask who did the work, what was measured, what was paid and whether it sat inside an arbodienst contract.

The opening offer is a €1,250 design-hurdle desk triage for one process, credited into a €4,750 design-hurdle baseline. Triage checks safety data sheets, task variants, existing RI&E evidence and certificates. The baseline adds the observed job, partner-reviewed quantitative assessment, action register and one Dutch management debrief. Laboratory analysis and extra measurement days are quoted after method selection and invoiced directly to the employer.

The owner or operations manager signs. The order names the process, normal job date, participating crew, hygienist, access to safety data sheets and the person who owns corrective actions. Fifty percent is paid before the visit. The current limits create a reason to inspect the old file; a customer audit, process change or actual Inspectorate visit creates priority.

The second kill fact is not settled online. ArboNed publicly sells blocks of prevention-expert time, including occupational hygiene, at €384 for two hours, €768 for four and €1,536 for eight. SGS asks prospects to request a quotation for exposure studies. These pages show paid capability, not a free bundle, but they reveal neither existing-customer contract terms nor a comparable diisocyanate price. Two incumbent quotes and contractor invoices must decide whether the niche is already included or undercut.

Year one is paid evidence. Six field jobs require roughly two founder days each plus selling and coordination. It fits beside a job only with booked site days and a partner who absorbs schedule variance. The loss after founder replacement is honest: the test does not replace developer income.

The mature model barely works. It needs one new baseline most weeks and more change reviews than baselines. There is no statutory annual renewal for the whole exposure file, so those reviews must be caused by real changes or voluntarily bought assurance. If average baseline revenue stays at the pitch's €1,500–€3,000 estimate, partner expertise and site labour consume the business.

Gross margin pays professional assessment, field delivery, instruments and travel before corporate overhead. Founder replacement is owner compensation, not free labour. The €30,000 remainder is company surplus and must absorb claims, idle days and hiring variance. A mature 49% gross margin is thin for work carrying professional liability.

Working capital stays light by billing half in advance, renting pumps, and putting laboratory and medical invoices outside the company. The year-one cash-at-risk design hurdle is €12,000 for insurance advice, partner deposits, training, travel and rentals. Spend no more than €4,500 before first payment. Do not buy a van, pump fleet or training platform. That keeps the experiment far below the €50,000 year-one ceiling.

From service to company

Customer one is founder work under the hygienist's method. The asset is a controlled evidence map: product, concentration, task, duration, control, worker group, model boundary, sample, result, action, owner and review trigger. Every gap records whether it belongs to the employer, supplier, hygienist, laboratory or physician.

Customer five must include a second spray trade and a second acquisition route. A trained technician can collect documents, calibrate rented instruments, observe an approved task and assemble records. The hygienist still chooses the method and interprets results. The founder handles qualification, unusual conditions and the management debrief. Half the routine hours should occur without the founder.

Customer twenty receives the same scope sheet, evidence request, task taxonomy, chain-of-custody record and report structure. The twentieth should arrive without a founder friendship. No hygienist, supplier or training provider should control more than a quarter of revenue. Customer records remain exportable files, not captive accounts in a portal.

The human work remains central. Someone must see whether a “ventilated booth” works as described, notice how a worker clears a nozzle and decide whether variation defeats the model. Delegation belongs in preparation, controlled observation, instrument handling and record assembly. The professional bottleneck remains method choice and interpretation.

The accumulating asset is a library of qualified task patterns, supplier-document mappings, bilingual interview prompts, sampling plans, common control failures and time data. It reduces field and hygienist hours on the next comparable process. If every report remains bespoke, or if the partner owns all methods and relationships, the twentieth delivery is the first delivery repeated. That is a subcontracting practice, not a company.

The incumbent gets a turn

SGS Search already markets exposure studies from target selection through pumps, laboratory analysis, interpretation and reporting. KWA describes the same four-step path, using a model where suitable and worker-worn pumps where it is not. ArboNed can add an occupational hygienist to an existing prevention relationship and publishes small blocks of expert time. A local entrant does not possess a missing technical capability.

The internal substitute may be enough. A prevention employee can maintain the product list and certificates, while a hygienist validates a model and appears only for exceptions. Suppliers already owe training material, and the five-year certificate makes training administration intermittent rather than monthly. A capable employer can therefore assemble the file from existing people and buy a specialist only when the model fails.

The partner has the strongest capture route. The hygienist owns credibility, method and Dutch management conversation. After the reader finds a Polish-speaking crew and organises the evidence, the partner can contract directly for the next review. A supplier can also refer only customers using its own system, narrowing independence. The defence is a multi-partner channel, controlled field method and customer contract. None is a deep moat.

The lead and chromium file is a later line, not support for this model. It adds medical coordination and different site types. Local-exhaust and respirator testing are also adjacent, but the reviewed Dutch record did not establish an interval that creates a recurring sale outside special regimes. Expansion should follow a paid request, not rescue weak diisocyanate economics.

The capable failure

The company executes well. Contractors like the bilingual site work, the hygienist approves sound assessments, and two measurements uncover correctable exposure. Then the portfolio separates. Most stable booth processes close with a model and an internal action list. The complex open-spray jobs require several representative days and senior interpretation, so the hygienist takes more of the fee.

Meanwhile customers copy the training register, put review triggers into their RI&E and call only after a material change. The five-year training cycle creates no annual retainer. The partner learns which crews value the work and offers them a wider occupational-health contract. Large providers spread travel and laboratory capacity across many hazards; the entrant carries idle equipment and fragmented site dates.

Nothing failed through careless selling or bad measurement. Good delivery taught stable customers to maintain the file, while difficult customers remained expert-heavy. The work survives as occasional bilingual field support to a hygienist. It may pay an attractive day rate, but fifty baselines and sixty reviews never appear. The model accepted by the Inspectorate did not eliminate work; it eliminated the repeatable middle.

The first ninety days

  1. Days 1–15: recruit two independent Dutch-speaking occupational hygienists. Ask each to mark the scope boundary, method decision, signature, insurance and customer ownership. Stop if neither will supervise controlled fieldwork by the reader.
  2. Days 1–25: build thirty accounts across the ten prospect types and conduct ten English, Polish or partner-led Dutch interviews. Ask for the last assessment, model, measurement, certificate audit, provider, invoice and trigger. Ask two training providers and two associations for aggregate firm and certificate counts; record refusals and incompatible definitions.
  3. Days 15–35: have one hygienist review the desk triage, evidence request and one dummy report. Obtain insurance advice and a rental quotation. Cap partner, legal and travel spending at a €2,000 pre-evidence hurdle.
  4. Days 25–55: offer the €1,250 desk triage to five qualified employers, 50% in advance. Also obtain comparable written quotes from two large providers, including what an existing arbodienst contract covers.
  5. Days 40–75: convert one triage into the €4,750 baseline. Observe a normal job beside the day job, with the hygienist's approved method. Rent instruments only if the assessment calls for measurement. Time founder, technician and hygienist work separately.
  6. Days 70–90: offer the same baseline to a second employer through a different route and quote the first real change review. Total spending before first customer payment is capped at €4,500.

Pass: two employers buy desk triage, one converts to a baseline, and a second accepts the baseline price through another route. The hygienist cost stays below one third of baseline revenue, and the first delivery identifies routine work a technician can repeat. Kill: no paid triage after five qualified offers; all five employers show a current accepted file inside an existing contract; comparable providers quote the full baseline below €3,000; or every credible hygienist insists on owning the customer. Also kill the direct route if Dutch management delivery cannot stay with the partner.

Verdict

TEST THROUGH A PARTNER

The hygienist is both the competence gate and the Dutch commercial layer. The single assumption most able to change this verdict is that two independent contractors will pay the mature €5,500 baseline hurdle while letting the entrant retain the customer and enough routine fieldwork to reach a 49% gross-margin design.
