# EU product and packaging obligations used in screening

The EU's CE guide says CE marking is mandatory only for products covered by harmonized rules; examples include toys, drones, electrical/electronic equipment, some PPE, machinery and radio equipment. CE must not be placed on products outside those rules.

The General Product Safety Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2023/988, applies from 2024-12-13 and addresses online sales and direct imports. The Cyber Resilience Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, applies generally from 2027-12-11; Article 14 incident/vulnerability reporting applies from 2026-09-11. The current consolidated text is the source for those dates.

The Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2025/40, began phased application on 2026-08-12 and applies to all packaging and packaging waste. Separate national producer-registration/EPR duties still need country-by-country operational handling. The toy guidance says manufacturers must assess chemical, physical, mechanical, electrical, flammability, hygiene and radioactivity hazards, conduct conformity assessment, and affix CE marking before EU sale under Directive 2009/48/EC.

For candidate scoping, connected radio products are flagged for Radio Equipment Directive 2014/53/EU, EMC Directive 2014/30/EU, RoHS Directive 2011/65/EU, WEEE Directive 2012/19/EU and, where batteries are included, Regulation (EU) 2023/1542. Mains equipment may also invoke Low Voltage Directive 2014/35/EU. The flag is triage, not a conformity opinion; the maker must provide design/test documentation and the final legal role allocation must be contracted.
